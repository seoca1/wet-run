Colin: Garage-Kabbalah

1274 wordsBridge Trilogy

"The garage was not a garage. The garage was a kind of temple. The garage was a place where Colin made the videos that the city could not make itself."

Hook

I am Colin. I make videos. I have made videos for ten years, in this garage, in this part of the city, with these people, with these cameras, with this kind of light. The videos I make are not the videos that the corporate-studios make. The videos I make are the videos that come out of the garage, that come out of the bridge-community, that come out of the attention-economy-substrate that the city below cannot understand.

The garage is on the second-tier east, in the part of the bridge that the Lost-Norters use as a kind of workshop. The garage has been my studio for ten years. The garage has been my temple for ten years. The garage has been the place where I do the only thing that I am good at.

Rising Action

I am good at making videos that show what the city looks like when the city is not looking. I am good at making videos that show what the bridge looks like when the bridge is being itself. I am good at making videos that show what the corporate-attention-economy looks like when the corporate-attention-economy thinks nobody is watching.

The videos are hard to make. The videos require equipment. The videos require cameras that the corporate-attention-economy has stopped manufacturing, because the corporate-attention-economy has decided that nobody wants analog-cameras anymore. The videos require film. The videos require the kind of film that the corporate-attention-economy has stopped producing, because the corporate-attention-economy has decided that nobody watches film anymore.

The videos also require the Rooster. The Rooster is my partner. The Rooster has been making videos with me for ten years. The Rooster is the one who figures out what the videos should show. The Rooster is the one who figures out what the city is trying to hide. The Rooster is the one who tells me where to point the camera.

Midpoint Reversal

But the Rooster has been sick. The Rooster has been sick for three weeks. The Rooster has been coughing, the kind of cough that means something is wrong with the body. The Rooster has been lying in his bed in the garage, in the corner that we use as a sleeping-corner, and he has been staring at the ceiling and he has been making sounds that are not words but that mean something to me.

The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that the bridge-community gets. The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that the Lost-Norters get. The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that comes from sleeping in places that are too cold, from breathing air that is too wet, from eating food that is too processed, from living a life that is too hard.

The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that does not get better on its own. The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that requires medicine. The Rooster's cough is the kind of cough that requires help.

Climax

But the garage-kabbalah was not just the Rooster's. The garage-kabbalah was also the bridge's. The garage-kabbalah was the bridge's way of making-itself-visible, the bridge's way of saying: this is what I look like when I am being myself. The Rooster had taught me that. The Rooster had taught me that the garage was not a garage but a kind of temple, the bridge's way of making-itself-visible. But the Rooster was sick now. But the Rooster was coughing now. But the temple needed a new-priest.

I made a video today, even though the Rooster could not help me. I made a video by myself, with the analog-camera, with the analog-film, with the bridge-community-watching-me-make-it. The video is a video of the bridge's third-tier at dawn. The video is a video of the bridge's cable-lights glowing in the fog. The video is a video of the city-lights reflecting off the Bay. The video is a video of the bridge being the bridge.

The video is not finished. The video needs editing. The video needs the Rooster to tell me what to cut and what to keep. The video needs the Rooster's attention-economy-reading to figure out what the video is trying to say.

But the Rooster cannot help me right now. The Rooster is coughing. The Rooster is sick. The Rooster is lying in his bed, staring at the ceiling, making sounds that are not words.

I went to the Lost-Norters' camp. I asked if anyone had medicine. The Lost-Norters said no. The Lost-Norters said the medicine-supply had run out three weeks ago. The Lost-Norters said the Armenians had been trying to get more medicine but the corporate-supply-chain had stopped shipping medicine to the bridge.

Resolution

I went back to the garage. I sat with the Rooster. I held his hand. The Rooster's hand was warm, the way the bridge's metal had been warm that night that Cordis had told me about, the night that the bridge had been humming.

The Rooster looked at me. The Rooster said something. The Rooster said: "Make the video. Make the video without me. Make the video that the city cannot make itself."

The Rooster said: "Make the video that shows what the bridge looks like when the bridge is being itself. Make the video that the corporate-attention-economy cannot make. Make the video that shows what is true."

I said: "I will."

The Rooster closed his eyes. The Rooster went back to coughing. The Rooster went back to staring at the ceiling. The Rooster went back to being sick.

The garage smelled of solder-fumes and old-video-equipment. The Rooster's cough was the kind of cough that the body makes when the body is fighting something that the body cannot name. I could feel the analog-camera's weight in my hands. I could feel the analog-film's warmth under my fingers. I could taste the metallic-tang of the bridge's air. I could hear the Lost-Norters moving through the third-tier above us. The garage-kabbalah was here. The truth was here.

"The garage was not a garage. The garage was a place where the truth could be made visible. The Rooster had taught me that. The Rooster was sick now. But the garage was still the garage."

I made the video. I made the video that the Rooster had asked me to make. I made the video that showed what the bridge looked like when the bridge was being itself.

The video was not as good as it would have been with the Rooster. But the video was true.

That was enough. That was what the garage was for.

The garage-kabbalah's practitioners included Colin, the Rooster, Chevette, and Skinner. Colin and the Rooster were the artists. Chevette and Skinner were the bridge-community witnesses. Together they formed the garage's ritual-circle, the kind of circle that the corporate-attention-economy could not understand.
