Molly's Extraction

A Razor Girl at the Edge of the Sprawl

257 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The hotel had a name. The hotel had been, in Molly's private count, the hotel of a kind she had used for thirty years, a kind that did not, in any municipal record, keep a register.

She checked the blades, blades of a kind no longer made, a kind a man in Chiba had installed for her in 1984.

The job was one she had done before.

The extraction had been, in the brief she had been given, an extraction of a man she had extracted once before, by a woman of a different name.

She sat on the bed. The bed had been placed, by some hotel-design logic, in a tatami room, a kind of room she had used in five cities.

She thought about the name. The name had been one she had not used in twelve years. The name had been the name of a woman who had worked in the Sprawl before the Sprawl had been, in any municipal record, called the Sprawl.

She put the blades back in their case, a case a man in Chiba had given her for a job she had done before.

She stood in the way she had been taught, the standing of a kind that did not, in any record she had been given, leave footprints.

She walked out.

The street was below, the street of a kind she had walked for thirty years, the kind of street that did not belong to anyone.

She walked.
