Laney: Cough-Syrup Recovery

800 wordsBridge Trilogy

Hook

"The attention-economy had used him up. Now he had to find out if there was anything left after the using."

Laney woke up on the bridge at four in the morning with a taste in his mouth like burnt-cotton and the knowledge that he had been asleep for nineteen hours. He lay very still for a minute, listening to his heartbeat, listening to the bridge-creak, listening to the small-sounds that the bridge made when nobody else was awake.

Then he sat up.

Rising Action

The cardboard-carton he had been sleeping in was damp from condensation. His jacket, which he had folded under his head, was damp too. The cough-syrup-bottle beside him was empty. He didn't remember drinking it, but he didn't remember not drinking it either. The line between the two had gotten blurry years ago.

He crawled out of the carton and stood up. His legs were stiff. His hands were shaking. His head felt like it had been filled with wet-sand and then the wet-sand had been replaced by something even heavier.

He walked to the bridge-rail and looked out at the Bay. The fog was thick, but he could see the lights of the city beyond it, orange-and-red, the corporate-spectrum, the attention-economy made visible.

He had been part of that economy. He had been a good-part, too, before the cough-syrup, before the Rooster's video-art, before the strange-attention-events that had burned out his cortex. He had been a finder, a pattern-reader, a person who could see what was about to happen in the attention-flow before it happened.

Midpoint Reversal

Then he had started taking the syrup to dampen the burn-out. Then the syrup had stopped working. Then he had taken more. Then more. Then he had ended up on the bridge, sleeping in cardboard-cartons, listening to the bridge-creak, trying to remember what it felt like to be a person with a future.

The Rooster was somewhere on the bridge too. Laney didn't want to think about the Rooster. The Rooster was the reason Laney had ended up here. The Rooster and his strange-video-art, his gaze-events, his way of making the attention-economy into something that could be looked-at.

But the cough-syrup was not the only thing Laney was giving up. The syrup had been his way of dampening the attention-economy-burnout; without it, the attention-flow would return in full-force, and Laney would have to learn to live in the attention-economy again, on the bridge's terms, not the corporate-terms. But for now, he was just a man who had thrown a bottle into the Bay.

Laney walked along the bridge-deck until he found a water-faucet someone had rigged up at the third-tier. He drank from it, the water cold and slightly-metallic, the kind of water that the Bay-Area ran through its pipes in the hours before dawn. He drank until his stomach hurt.

Then he walked back to his carton and sat down. He looked at the empty syrup-bottle. He looked at the bridge-rail. He looked at the city-lights beyond the fog.

Climax

He had a choice. He could walk down the bridge's western-ramp, into the city, into the corporate-attention-economy that had used him up. He could find a new-boss, a new-job, a new-round of finding-patterns-and-burning-out. He could go back to what he had been.

Or he could stay. He could stay on the bridge. He could learn to live in the in-between. He could find a way to be a person with a future that wasn't the corporate-future.

He sat there for a long time, looking at the empty-bottle, looking at the fog, listening to the bridge-creak.

Then he picked up the bottle and threw it over the rail, into the Bay. It made a small-sound when it hit the water, a tinkle-and-plop, and then it was gone.

Resolution

He sat there for another minute. Then he stood up and walked toward the eastern-tier of the bridge, where the Black Travelers had their community-kitchen, where the food was always hot, where nobody asked questions.

Behind him, the empty-space where the bottle had been held the dawn-light for a moment. Then the light moved on. Then the bridge-creak sounded again.

The bridge was patient. The bridge had seen a thousand people like him. The bridge knew what to do.

Laney didn't know what to do. But the bridge did. He could trust that.

"Some people come to the bridge because they have nowhere else to go. Some people come to the bridge because they have to learn to go nowhere. Laney was both kinds."
