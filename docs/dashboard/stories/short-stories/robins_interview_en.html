Robin's Interview

A War Photographer Asks the Bridge Three Questions

268 wordsBridge Trilogy

The studio was above the south anchorage. The studio had been a dry-cleaner's shop. The studio had not been a dry-cleaner's shop for six years.

Robin had been in the studio for forty minutes before the interviewer had arrived.

The interviewer was a young man. The interviewer had a phone. The phone was the same shape as the tape recorder he had been told to bring.

"Tell me about the bridge," the interviewer said.

Robin did not answer.

"Tell me what you saw."

Robin set down the cup. The cup was a chipped enamel cup. The chip had been a chip in the cup for longer than the interviewer had been alive.

"I saw children," Robin said. "I saw children who had slept on the bridge."

The interviewer waited.

"I saw Chevette before Chevette was Chevette. I saw Chevette when Chevette was a different name."

"I saw a man who had been a Loveless. The man had stopped being a Loveless because the bridge had stopped being a thing that Lovelesses could be."

The interviewer looked up.

"Three questions," Robin said. "I have answered three questions. I do not give a fourth."

"I do not give a fourth because the fourth is mine. The fourth is the question I have asked myself. The fourth is the question I have not answered."

Robin stood up. Robin crossed to the window. Robin looked out at the bridge.

"I will not describe the bridge. The bridge has been described. The bridge has been described by the people who live on it. The bridge has been described by the people who have died on it."

The interview was over.
