Bridge Market: The Corral Protocol

Hook

The smart-matter was wrong.

I knew it the way I know when Skinner's hands are cold: from a distance, by the way the air felt against them. Two weeks earlier, the Armenians had rolled a pallet of folded T-shirts onto the bridge's lower-deck for the night-market, and I'd helped Chevette — me, Chevette, same body, same fixer, same day-old hair — sort them into colors under the sodium-lamp on the suspension-side. The smart-matter had come in on the same kind of pallet, shrink-wrapped, three bundles, each the size and approximate mass of a rolled futon. The driver was a woman I didn't know with a plastic eye and a Cossack hat, which sounds more picturesque than it was, because the Cossack hat was missing its crown and the plastic eye looked out at a different angle than the real one. She had handed me a slip of paper and a key, and I'd signed the slip, and she'd walked back down to the on-ramp and into a pickup-truck that somebody had welded an extra cab onto in back, and the pickup-truck had pulled away toward Treasure Island, and I'd thought no more about it.

The key was supposed to open one of the three bundles. I had been told which one by the woman with the plastic eye, who had told me in the way people tell you things on the bridge, which is to say by pointing at a man who was standing on the suspension-cable's third-tier, smoking, and saying him and then first. I'd asked the man on the cable if he was with the smart-matter. He'd said yes. I'd asked if the first bundle was his. He'd said yes. I'd given him the key. He'd climbed down. That had been the protocol. That had been two weeks ago.

Tonight I was going to open the second bundle.

The reason I was going to open the second bundle was that the man on the cable had not come back for the second bundle in two weeks, and the woman with the plastic eye had not come back either, and the Armenians had asked me, very politely, very Tuesday, whether the second bundle was still there, because if it was still there, then the smart-matter was not moving, and if the smart-matter was not moving, then somebody was about to lose a number of dollars that would make the bridge's economy of bicycle-tires and hash-oil and recycled aluminet look like a lemonade-stand. I had said yes, the bundle was still there. They had said Tuesday, sister, and they had walked away. And I had walked down to the lower-deck, where the smart-matter was stacked in a corner of the corral I had inherited from Skinner two years ago when Skinner had decided he was too old to manage the night-market's paperwork, and I had knelt in front of the second bundle and run my thumb along the seam, and the seam had been wrong.

The seam had been wrong because the bundle was supposed to be smart-matter, which is to say programmable, which is to say it would have a haptic-finish and a slight warmth to it and a faint field you could feel in your wrist if you held your hand close to it for a long time, the way a magnet's field felt in the old stories. The bundle was cold. The bundle was the temperature of a body that had been left in the bay. The bundle was the wrong temperature, and that was how I knew, before I cut the shrink-wrap, that something had been added to the smart-matter, or that something had been taken out of it, or that something had been substituted for it entirely, because the people who make smart-matter do not make cold smart-matter, the way the people who make watches do not make watches that don't keep time.

I went to find Skinner.

Rising Action

Skinner was on the upper-tier, in his cubby, asleep.

I didn't wake him. I sat on the deck-planking outside his cubby for maybe ten minutes, listening to him snore and listening to the bridge and watching the fog move through the suspension-cables in slow-billows, the way the smoke from a hookah moves in the cartoons the Lost-Norters played on the projector they had stolen from a hotel that had gone out of business. Then a kid came up the catwalk from the lower-deck. The kid was maybe eleven. The kid was Maya, who was the daughter of a woman named Rita who sold knock-off OshKosh from a folding-table on the suspension-side four nights a week, and Maya was the kind of kid who never went anywhere without a tote-bag, and tonight the tote-bag was a different tote-bag, a small one, a kid-sized one, a tote-bag that had not been on Maya's shoulder at the start of the evening.

Maya sat down next to me. I didn't say anything. She didn't say anything. We both listened to Skinner snore for a minute. Then Maya said, I think I stole something, Chevette.

I said, Show me.

Maya opened the tote-bag. Inside was a flat-disc of smart-matter, the size and approximate thickness of a saucer, the color of wet-ash. It was warm. It was humming. It was the right kind of smart-matter, the kind I'd been expecting, the kind with the haptic-finish and the field in the wrist. Maya had taken it out of the second bundle, the bundle I had been about to open, the bundle the man on the cable had not come back for. Maya had opened the bundle. Maya had taken the disc. Maya had carried it to the upper-tier in a tote-bag.

I said, Where did you get that, Maya.

Maya said, Out of the big white thing, by the wall. By the thing you said not to touch.

I said, Why did you touch it, Maya.

Maya said, Because I thought it was for me.

The way she said it made me believe her. She had not stolen it to sell. She had not stolen it to trade. She had stolen it because, in the economy of a kid on the bridge, in the moral-economy of a kid who has been told her whole life that nothing on the bridge is for her, the appearance of a thing that is humming is the appearance of a thing that might be for her. The disc was warm. The disc was hers in the way that warmth is a child's, before warmth is anything else.

I said, Did anyone see you take it, Maya.

Maya said, I don't know. I don't think so.

I said, Were there other people around the big white thing.

Maya said, There's always people around the big white thing, Chevette. It's the smart-matter. It's the wrong-temperature one. People have been talking about it. The Armenians have been talking about it. The Black Travelers have been talking about it. There's a man who's been sitting in the shadows by the corral since yesterday who looks like he's waiting for somebody.

I said, Tuesday, sister.

Maya said, What do I do.

I said, You give it to me. You go to Rita's table. You sit with Rita. You do not come back to the corral tonight. You do not touch the big white thing again. You do not talk about this with anyone except Rita. And you do not tell Rita what it is, you tell her you lost a tote-bag and you found another one and you're going to return it to the Armenians, who you do, and they will handle it, and you will get a tot-bag credit for it tomorrow, which Rita will know how to apply.

Maya handed me the disc. The disc was warm in my hand. The disc was humming, low, the way a refrigerator hums, but softer, the way a sleeping cat hums, but colder, the way a sleeping-something-that-is-not-a-cat hums. The disc was smart-matter. The disc was the kind of smart-matter that was worth more than every bicycle-tire and every gram of hash-oil and every square-meter of aluminet in the bridge's whole night-market-economy, by a factor of about a hundred-thousand. The disc was the kind of smart-matter that would not be on the bridge, by any honest protocol, because the bridge is not where valuable things are stored. The bridge is where valuable things pass through. The bridge is a corridor. The bridge is not a vault. The fact that the disc was here, in Maya's tote-bag, was the fact that was going to be a problem, and the fact that was going to be a problem because of the two factions Maya had mentioned, the Armenians and the Black Travelers, both of which had been eyeing the bundle for the past two weeks and both of which had been waiting for the bundle to either open itself or be opened by the man on the cable, who was not the man on the cable anymore because the man on the cable had disappeared, and the woman with the plastic eye had disappeared, and the man in the shadows had appeared, and the bundle was still here, and now the bundle was also open, and a kid had taken the disc, and the kid had given the disc to me.

I put the disc in my jacket-pocket. The disc sat against my ribs. I could feel it through the canvas, through the bar-coded Allied badges on the lapels, through the horsehide Skinner's old jacket was made of. The disc was warm. The disc was humming. The disc was, I now realized, transmitting.

I went down to the corral.

Midpoint Reversal

The man in the shadows was not in the shadows anymore.

He was standing at the corral's entrance, which was not an entrance but a gap in the aluminet that Skinner had hung across two I-beams to make the corral look like a room. The man was tall. The man was thin. The man was wearing a coat I had not seen before, a long-coat, dark, with a sheen to it that was not quite silk and not quite leather and not quite the kind of thing the bridge people wore, which is to say it was the kind of thing the people from off the bridge wore, the people from the city, the people from the city who came up to the bridge to do things they did not want to do in the city.

The man looked at me. I looked at the man.

The man said, You have it.

I said, I don't know what you're talking about.

The man said, The disc, Chevette. The disc. I know Maya took it. I know you have it. I'm not here to hurt you. I'm here to take it back to the people who own it, and then I'm going to leave, and then you're going to have a very boring week.

I said, I don't know any Maya.

The man smiled. The man's smile was the kind of smile that comes from a long time in a profession that requires smiling, the kind of smile that is a tool and a wound at the same time. The man said, You don't have to know any Maya. The disc is what I want. I will pay you for the disc. I will pay you more than the Armenians will pay you. I will pay you more than the Black Travelers will pay you. I will pay you more than anyone on the bridge will pay you, because the people who own the disc have more money than everyone on the bridge combined, and they have decided that tonight, on the bridge, the disc will go back to its owners, and the rest of the night will be a non-event.

I said, What are the people who own the disc.

The man said, The kind of people you do not want to know about, Chevette. The kind of people whose names are in the kind of files that get opened by the kind of people whose names are in the kind of files that you do not want opened. The kind of people who own a disc.

I thought about Skinner. I thought about Maya. I thought about the Armenians, who had asked me about the smart-matter, very politely, very Tuesday, and who would, in three days, ask me again, and in three days after that, and in three days after that, until the question was not a question but a request, and the request was not a request but a demand, and the demand was not a demand but a consequence. The Armenians were not the kind of people whose names were in the kind of files. The Armenians were the kind of people whose names were on the kind of menus at the kind of restaurants that did not have menus, the kind of restaurants that were also not restaurants but were also not not-restaurants but were a particular kind of place that exists in the bay-area, in the in-between places, in the places where the city ends and the bridge begins.

I said, What's in the disc.

The man said, Nothing you would understand.

I said, Try me.

The man looked at me. The man looked at the bridge. The man looked at the corral. The man looked at the bundle, which was still on the pallet, still shrink-wrapped except for the seam Maya had opened, which I had not yet re-sealed, which I was going to re-seal tonight, after I had figured out what the disc was, after I had figured out who the man was, after I had figured out what to do with the disc, which was warm against my ribs, which was humming against my ribs, which was, I now realized, transmitting a signal, a low signal, a signal that I could feel in my wrist the way I had been told you could feel a smart-matter's field, the way I had not been able to feel a smart-matter's field for the past two weeks because the bundle had been the wrong temperature, because the bundle had been the wrong smart-matter, because the bundle had been a decoy, because the disc had been inside the bundle the whole time, and the disc had been the actual smart-matter, and the bundle had been the decoy, and the decoy had been for the people who had not known the disc was inside it, and the man in the shadows had known, and the man in the shadows had been waiting for someone to open the bundle, and Maya had opened the bundle, and now the man in the shadows was here, and the man in the shadows was smiling his tool-and-wound smile.

The man said, The disc is a key, Chevette. The disc is a key to a vault in Oakland. The vault belongs to a woman who used to live on this bridge. The woman was Lovelock. Lovelock was a courier. Lovelock died two years ago. The vault is what Lovelock left behind. The disc is what opens the vault. The people who own the disc are the people who want to know what is in the vault, and they have decided that they want to know tonight.

I said, Who are the people who own the disc.

The man said, The kind of people who would not like the Armenians, Chevette. The kind of people who would not like the Black Travelers. The kind of people who would not like the bridge, except for the parts of the bridge that are useful to them, and the bridge is, tonight, useful to them, because of the disc, and because of you, and because of Maya, who is a child, and who should not be holding a disc, and who is not, anymore, holding a disc, because you are holding the disc, and you are going to give me the disc.

I said, The Armenians will not like this.

The man said, The Armenians will never know.

I said, The Black Travelers will not like this.

The man said, The Black Travelers will never know.

I said, The bridge will not like this.

The man said, The bridge will never know.

I said, I will know.

The man said, You will forget.

I said, Try me.

The man stopped smiling. The man took a step toward me. The man reached into his coat and took out a phone, not a smartphone, a real phone, a phone made of plastic, a phone that had a cord. The man held the phone up so I could see the screen. The screen was showing a picture of Maya. Maya was at Rita's table. Rita was not at Rita's table. Rita was at the bottom of the catwalk, with a man in another long-coat, and the man in the other long-coat had his hand on Rita's arm, and Rita was not moving.

The man said, The Armenians will not like this. The Black Travelers will not like this. The bridge will not like this. I do not like this, Chevette. I am a man who solves problems. You are a problem. The disc is a problem. Maya is not a problem. Rita is not a problem. You are the problem. Give me the disc and the problem is solved.

I took the disc out of my pocket. I held the disc in my hand. The disc was warm. The disc was humming. The disc was, I now realized, not transmitting. The disc was receiving. The disc was receiving a signal that was being sent from somewhere on the bridge, and the signal was being sent by a thing that the disc was designed to talk to, and the thing was, I now realized, in the bundle, which was not a decoy, which was a receiver, which was the other half of a pair, the way a phone is the other half of a phone, the way a key is the other half of a lock, the way a bridge is the other half of a river.

I put the disc back in my pocket.

Climax

The man in the long-coat moved. The man was fast, the way a person is fast when they have been trained to be fast, the way a person is fast when they have been trained not to be a person but to be a tool. The man's hand was at my collar. The man's other hand was at my jacket-pocket. The man's face was close to my face. The man's breath smelled of menthol and something chemical, something industrial, the kind of thing the refineries leaked at night when nobody was checking scrubbers.

I said, Tuesday.

The man paused. The man heard the word. The word was a bridge-word. The word was the bridge-word for: I am the bridge. The bridge is me. You are on the bridge. The bridge is on you.

The man said, I don't speak bridge.

I said, You're on the bridge.

The man said, I am on the bridge. The bridge does not own me.

I said, The bridge owns everyone who is on the bridge.

The man smiled again. The man's smile was not the tool-and-wound smile. The man's smile was the smile of a man who had been told, by the people who had trained him, that bridges do not own people, that bridges are pieces of infrastructure, that bridges are made of steel and concrete and do not have opinions, that bridges are not alive.

I said, You're wrong.

I bit the man's hand.

The man made a sound that was not a word. The man took his hand out of my collar. The man's hand had teeth-marks in it. The man was bleeding. The blood was red, the kind of red that is the color of blood when blood is fresh, the kind of red that smart-matter was supposed to be, the kind of red that the disc was not, because the disc was the color of wet-ash.

I ran.

I ran the way I run when I am running from a man who is fast: by going to a place where a fast man cannot be fast, which is, on the bridge, the catwalk under the deck at the next-bay west, where the catwalk is narrow and the man would have to duck, and the man would have to duck slowly, and I would be gone. I went to the catwalk. I was on the catwalk. The catwalk was narrow. The catwalk was under the deck. The catwalk smelled of creosote and the cold of the bay, the way a thing that is under a bridge smells of the bridge and the bay at the same time, the way a thing that is between two things smells of both.

I ran along the catwalk. I ran past the next-bay. I ran past the next-next-bay. I ran to the place where the catwalk went up to the upper-deck, where the cubbies were, where Skinner's cubby was, where Skinner was still asleep, where Skinner would still be asleep, because Skinner sleeps the way Skinner has always slept, the way the bridge sleeps, the way a thing that is between things sleeps.

I went into Skinner's cubby. I said, Skinner.

Skinner opened one eye. Skinner said, Tuesday, Chevette.

I said, There is a man. The man is fast. The man wants the disc. The man has Maya. The man has Rita.

Skinner sat up. Skinner said, What disc.

I took the disc out of my pocket. I showed Skinner the disc. The disc was warm. The disc was humming. The disc was receiving.

Skinner said, That's a Lovelock disc.

I said, I know.

Skinner said, Lovelock's been dead two years.

I said, I know.

Skinner said, How did Maya get it.

I said, Maya took it. Maya thought it was for her.

Skinner said, Maya's a good kid.

I said, Maya is a kid. The man is not a good man. The man has Maya. The man has Rita.

Skinner stood up. Skinner was shorter than I remembered. Skinner was older than I remembered. Skinner's face was the face of a man who had lived on the bridge for thirty years and who had been, in another life, a man who had done other things in other places, the kind of other-things that the bridge does not remember, the kind of other-things that are written in the body of a man in a way that the bridge does not read.

Skinner said, The man is one of them. The man is from the city. The man is from the kind of city that has files. The man is from the kind of city that has a disc. The man is from the kind of city that does not know what the bridge is.

I said, The man is in the corral. The man is bleeding. The man is waiting for me to come back.

Skinner said, The man is not waiting. The man is calling.

I heard the man calling. The man's voice was on the upper-deck, in the corral, on the phone, the real phone, the phone with the cord. The man was talking to someone. The man was talking about a disc. The man was talking about a vault. The man was talking about Oakland. The man was talking about Lovelock. The man was talking about me.

Skinner said, Chevette. You have a choice.

I said, I know.

Skinner said, You can give the man the disc. The man will leave. The man will take Maya. The man will take Rita. The man will go to Oakland. The man will open the vault. The man will give the people who own the disc whatever is in the vault. The people who own the disc will be happy. The man will be paid. Maya will be returned. Rita will be returned. The bridge will not be involved. The bridge will not be a problem. The bridge will be a corridor, which is what the city thinks the bridge is.

I said, And.

Skinner said, And you can keep the disc. The man will not leave. The man will not take Maya. The man will not take Rita. The man will wait. The man will call more men. The men will come to the bridge. The men will be from the city. The men will be from the kind of city that has files. The men will be from the kind of city that does not know what the bridge is. The men will try to take the disc. The men will try to take you. The men will try to take Maya. The men will try to take Rita. The men will try to take the bridge.

I said, And.

Skinner said, And I will be here. And Chevette will be here. And the Armenians will be here. And the Black Travelers will be here. And the bridge will be here. And the bridge will be a vault, which is what the bridge is, which is what the bridge has always been, which is what the bridge has always been when the city has tried to make the bridge a corridor.

I said, The bridge is a vault.

Skinner said, The bridge is a vault. The bridge has been a vault for fifty years. The bridge was a vault when Lovelock was alive. The bridge was a vault when Lovelock's vault in Oakland was full. The bridge is a vault now, when Lovelock's vault is the disc.

I said, Lovelock is dead.

Skinner said, Lovelock is dead. The disc is not dead. The vault is not dead. The bridge is not dead. The bridge is what Lovelock left, and what Lovelock left is a vault, and the disc is the key, and the key is in your pocket, and the man is in the corral, and the man is calling his men.

I said, What do I do.

Skinner said, You do what Lovelock would have done. You do what Chevette would have done. You do what Skinner would have done. You do what the bridge would have done. You do what the bridge has always done. You give the man a Tuesday he will not forget.

I went to the corral.

Resolution

The man in the long-coat was sitting on the bundle. The bundle was the wrong-temperature bundle. The man was bleeding. The man was on the phone. The man saw me. The man smiled. The man's smile was not the tool-and-wound smile. The man's smile was the smile of a man who had been told that bridges do not own people and who was beginning, in the way that people begin to believe things they have been told, to believe it.

I said, The disc is Lovelock's. The disc is the bridge's. The disc is mine. The disc is Maya's. The disc is Rita's. The disc is not yours.

The man said, I will pay you.

I said, You will not pay me. You will leave. You will leave Maya. You will leave Rita. You will leave the bridge. You will leave the disc. You will leave.

The man said, I cannot leave. The people who own the disc are the kind of people who do not leave when they are told to leave. The people who own the disc are the kind of people who leave when the disc is in their hands.

I said, The disc is not in your hands.

The man said, The disc is not in my hands. The disc is in your hands. The disc is in your pocket. The disc is what you have. The disc is what I want. The disc is what the people who own the disc want.

I said, The people who own the disc are not here.

The man said, The people who own the disc are always here.

I said, The people who own the disc are on the bridge. The people who own the disc are the Armenians. The people who own the disc are the Black Travelers. The people who own the disc are Skinner. The people who own the disc are Chevette. The people who own the disc are Maya. The people who own the disc are Rita. The people who own the disc are the people who are on the bridge. The people who own the disc are the people who live on the bridge. The people who own the disc are the people who are the bridge.

The man did not say anything. The man looked at the bridge. The man looked at the corral. The man looked at the bundle. The man looked at the aluminet. The man looked at the suspension-cables, the way the suspension-cables looked in the fog, the way everything looks in the fog, the way the bridge is the bridge in the fog.

I took the disc out of my pocket. I held the disc in my hand. The disc was warm. The disc was humming. The disc was receiving.

I put the disc in the bundle. I re-sealed the bundle. The bundle was the wrong temperature. The bundle was the temperature of a body that had been left in the bay. The bundle was the temperature of a disc that was no longer receiving. The bundle was the temperature of a key that was in a lock, and the lock was the bridge, and the bridge was a vault, and the vault was Lovelock's, and Lovelock was dead, and the disc was Lovelock's, and the disc was the bridge's, and the disc was mine, and the disc was Maya's, and the disc was Rita's, and the disc was in the bundle, and the bundle was in the corral, and the corral was on the bridge, and the bridge was on the bay, and the bay was on the Pacific, and the Pacific was on the world, and the world was the bridge, and the bridge was the world, and the world was a vault, and the vault was the world, and the disc was in the vault, and the man was in the corral, and the man was on the bridge, and the man was in the world, and the man was not leaving, but the disc was not leaving, and Maya was not leaving, and Rita was not leaving, and the bridge was not leaving, and the bridge was not a corridor, and the bridge was a vault, and the man was in the vault, and the man was on the bridge, and the man was not a problem, and the man was a Tuesday, and the Tuesday was a word, and the word was the bridge, and the bridge was a word, and the word was a vault, and the vault was a bridge, and the bridge was Lovelock, and Lovelock was the bridge, and Lovelock was not dead, and Lovelock was the disc, and the disc was in the bundle, and the bundle was in the corral, and the corral was on the bridge, and the bridge was the world.

The man left.

The man left Maya at Rita's table. The man left Rita at the bottom of the catwalk. The man left the disc in the bundle. The man left the bridge on the on-ramp, in a car that was not a car, that was a thing, that was the kind of thing the people from the city drove, the kind of thing that had files in it, the kind of thing that was not the bridge.

I sat in the corral for a long time. I sat in the corral with the bundle. I sat in the corral with the disc. I sat in the corral with Skinner, who had come down from the upper-tier, who had brought two cups of Armenian coffee, who had handed me one, who had sat on the bundle next to me, who had looked at the bridge, who had looked at the fog, who had not said anything for a long time.

Then Skinner said, The bridge is a vault.

I said, The bridge is a vault.

Skinner said, Lovelock knew that.

I said, Lovelock knew that.

Skinner said, You know that now.

I said, I know that now.

Skinner said, Maya will be okay. Rita will be okay. The Armenians will be okay. The Black Travelers will be okay. The man will be okay. The city will be okay. The bridge will be okay. The disc will be okay. The vault will be okay. The disc will be in the vault. The vault will be in the bridge. The bridge will be in the world.

I said, And the world will be in the bridge.

Skinner said, The world has always been in the bridge, Chevette. The world has always been in the bridge. The bridge is the world. The bridge is what the world is. The bridge is what the world has always been. The bridge is the world that the world has always been.

We sat in the corral. We drank the coffee. The coffee was hot. The coffee was strong. The coffee was the kind of coffee the Armenians made, the kind of coffee that was made in a small copper-pot on a small burner on a small table on the bridge, the kind of coffee that smelled of cardamom and the cold of the bay.

The bridge was quiet. The bridge was a vault. The bridge was the world.

I finished the coffee. Skinner finished the coffee. We put the cups on the bundle. We walked up to the upper-tier. We went to our cubbies. We went to sleep.

The bridge was the bridge. The bridge was the vault. The bridge was the world. The bridge was the world that the world had always been.

I slept.

The bridge held me.

The disc was in the bundle. The bundle was in the corral. The corral was on the bridge. The bridge was on the bay. The bay was on the world.

The world was the bridge.

The bridge was the world.

Derivative short-story for the Bridge Trilogy. Chevette Washington — the bicycle-courier protagonist of Virtual Light — is on the bridge's lower-deck when a smart-matter shipment that has been waiting two weeks for its owner turns out to contain a Lovelock-disc, a key to a vault in Oakland left behind by the courier Lovelock before her death. A child (Maya) takes the disc; a man from the city comes to take it back. Chevette must decide whether to hand the disc to the city (and let the bridge be a corridor) or keep it on the bridge (and let the bridge be a vault). She chooses the bridge. The protocol is a Tuesday — the bridge-word for: I am the bridge.
