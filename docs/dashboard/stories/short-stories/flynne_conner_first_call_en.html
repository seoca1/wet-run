First Caller: Conner on the Line

Origin: Flynne's first call from Conner Penske

804 wordsPeripheral

The phone rang at four-eleven in the afternoon and I let it ring four times because the kitchen sink was running and Burton had told me twice already that the phone was going to ring one of these days from a number I did not recognize and I was to pick it up on the second ring at the latest. I had been picking it up on the fourth ring because I had been testing whether Burton was right. Burton had been right. The phone was ringing.

I turned off the sink.

I picked up the handset.

The plastic of the handset was warm from the sun coming through the kitchen window, which it never should have been, the kitchen window faced north. There was no sun coming through the kitchen window.

"Hello," I said.

"Ms. Fisher." The voice was male, young, with a kind of careful diction that I had only ever heard in period dramas on the public feed. "My name is Conner. Conner Penske. I have the honor of being the person whose number your brother has been telling you about."

"My brother has been telling me a lot of things."

"Yes. I'm sure he has. I'm the one who is going to be telling you things in the future, as it were. I'm calling to introduce myself before we meet in the peripheral."

"In the peripheral."

"In the body. In the person. In the future-London-body-that-is-not-yours. We haven't met there yet. We will."

I sat down on the kitchen stool. The stool wobbled. The third basin cup of the kitchen sink was dripping into a pan that I had been meaning to empty for two days, and the drip had the rhythm of a metronome set to something slightly slower than resting heart rate.

"Why are you calling me on a phone," I said.

"Because phones are what you have."

"You are calling me from the future on a phone that I own in the past. That's what you are telling me."

"That's what I'm telling you."

"How does a phone call from the future get to a phone in the past."

There's a pause. A pause that was not a silence. There was noise behind his pause, a faint hum like the noise of a city at distance, like the noise of a generator running behind a building you couldn't quite see.

"Ms. Fisher, the honest answer is that I do not entirely understand it myself. The technical answer involves servers and what your brother's people call a clive, and a kind of probability that I'm not sure how to translate to the period. The practical answer is that someone on our end has set it up so that when I dial this number, it rings on your phone, in your kitchen, on a Tuesday afternoon in the year you are in."

"The year I am in."

"Yes."

"And what year am I in."

"You are in whatever year you are in, Ms. Fisher. The call is being placed from a year that I will tell you when we meet in the peripheral."

"You won't tell me now."

"I won't tell you now."

"Conner."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"You are calling from the future on my kitchen phone to introduce yourself, and you won't tell me what year you are in."

"I am calling from a time that is not yet your present, on a phone that is your present, in order to introduce myself so that when you and I meet in the body that is also not yet your present, you will recognize me. And I will not tell you the year because the year is information that I am not authorized to give you yet. I am authorized to introduce myself. I am authorized to say that I am glad we will be working together. I am authorized to say that I will see you in the peripheral."

"The body that is not yet mine."

"Yes."

"And you won't tell me when."

"Not yet."

I looked at the kitchen sink. The third basin dripped into the pan. The drip had the rhythm of a metronome set to something slightly faster than resting heart rate now, or maybe I was the one whose heart rate had changed.

"Conner."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"I will see you in the peripheral."

"You will see me in the peripheral, Ms. Fisher."

"Tell them to fix the drip in my kitchen sink."

"I'm sorry?"

"Tell whoever on your end arranged this phone call. The third basin drips into a pan. The drip has been driving me crazy since last Tuesday. If you can call from the future, you can fix a sink."

I hung up.

The kitchen was quiet.

The phone did not ring again that afternoon.

The third basin dripped into the pan.
