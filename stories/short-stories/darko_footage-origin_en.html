Darko: Footage Origin

1180 wordsBlue Ant Trilogy

Hook

"Darko had not meant to post the footage. Darko had not meant to start a thousand-person-substrate-emergence. Darko had just been in the plaza, pointing a camera at a girl."

Darko was in his apartment in Moscow, looking at the footage of the girl walking across the plaza, and trying to remember what he had been thinking when he had pointed the camera at her.

He had been in the plaza because he was always in the plaza. He lived nearby. He had a small-business that did not require him to be anywhere at any specific-time, and the plaza was the kind of place where a person could sit for hours without anyone asking him why.

He had pointed the camera at the girl because she had been there, and because she was the kind of person who, when she walked across a plaza, made the plaza look like a place that something was about to happen in. She had not been doing anything. She had not been carrying anything. She had not been looking at anyone.

Rising Action

She had just been walking.

But the way she had been walking had been the kind of walking that Darko's camera wanted to capture. Darko had been making footage for years. He had made footage of buildings, of streets, of weather, of dogs, of strangers. He had never made footage that anyone had wanted to watch more than once.

Until the girl in the plaza.

He had posted the footage to a forum he sometimes used, a small-forum for amateur-footage-makers. He had not expected anyone to notice. He had not expected the footage to be picked-up by another-forum, and another-forum, and another-forum. He had not expected the footage to become the kind of substrate-emergence that it had become.

He had especially not expected Cayce Pollard.

Midpoint Reversal

Cayce had contacted him through the forum, through a series of intermediaries, through a chain of people who knew people who knew people. Cayce had wanted to know if Darko had been hired to make the footage. Cayce had wanted to know if the footage was a piece of corporate-commission-work. Cayce had wanted to know if the girl in the footage was a real-girl or a paid-actor or a CGI-construct.

The answer was no. The answer was that Darko had pointed a camera at a girl because the girl was the kind of girl that cameras wanted to point at. The answer was that the footage was not a thing that anyone had made. The footage was just a piece of the world.

Cayce had not believed him. Or, more precisely, Cayce had believed him but had not been satisfied. Cayce had wanted the footage to be a thing. Cayce had wanted the footage to have a source, an origin, a meaning. Cayce had wanted the footage to be a substrate-emergence with a substrate-emergence's typical-structure: a person, a place, a moment, a meaning.

But Darko was not the source. The source was the girl in the footage. The girl had been the substrate-emergence. The girl had walked across the plaza with purpose, and the purpose had been visible to anyone with a camera and an attention-to-substrate. But Darko had not understood what he had captured. He had just captured it. He had just pointed a camera and pressed a button. The footage had done the rest. The footage had become what it was without him.

But Darko had been there, and Darko had pointed the camera, and there had been no meaning. There had just been a girl walking across a plaza, and a man with a camera, and the camera's desire to capture the girl.

Darko did not think this was interesting. Darko thought this was just the way the world worked. Things happened. Cameras captured things. Footage was made. People watched footage. Some footage became substrate-emergences. Most footage did not.

Climax

The girl in the footage had been a substrate-emergence because something about her had been the kind of thing that the world wanted to see. Darko did not know what that thing was. He did not think anyone knew what that thing was. He thought the world wanted to see it because the world was the kind of world that wanted to see things, and the girl in the footage had been the kind of thing that the world wanted to see.

He had tried to explain this to Cayce. Cayce had listened. Cayce had not been satisfied.

Then Cayce had asked him a question. The question was: "Do you know what the girl in the footage is walking toward?"

Darko had thought about this. He had looked at the footage again, frame by frame, the way Cayce had been looking at it. He had watched the girl walk across the plaza. He had watched the crowd part. He had watched the light change.

He had not known what the girl was walking toward. He had not known what the girl was walking away from.

Resolution

But he had known one thing. The girl had been walking somewhere. The girl had been walking with purpose. The girl had been walking as if she knew where she was going.

That was the footage's-substrate. Not the girl. Not the plaza. Not the light. The substrate was the girl's-purpose. The substrate was the way she walked, the way she moved, the way she knew where she was going.

And that was the thing that Darko could not explain to Cayce. Because Darko had pointed the camera at the girl not because of who she was, but because of how she walked. Darko had pointed the camera at the girl's-purpose. The girl herself had been incidental.

Darko did not know what to do with this knowledge. The knowledge did not help him. The knowledge was not the kind of knowledge that could be sold.

But the knowledge was the substrate. The knowledge was what the footage was.

"Darko had been the first-watcher. Darko had been the second-watcher. Darko had been the only-watcher, until the world decided to watch with him. And then the world had asked him what the footage was, and Darko had said: I don't know. I just knew how she walked."

The footage kept playing on his laptop. The girl walked across the plaza. The crowd parted. The light changed.

Darko felt the cold air-conditioning on his neck, the soft-fabric of his chair against his back, the smell of coffee from somewhere in the apartment. He could hear the refrigerator humming in the kitchen. The footage's sound was gone now; there was only the apartment-sound, the city-sound outside the window, the sound of the world that had not been captured. The footage had taken the world's-sound and given back nothing; the footage was a silent-substrate-emergence. Darko thought about that. He tasted something metallic in his mouth. He swallowed. He closed the laptop and went to make more coffee.

Darko watched it again. Then he closed the laptop and went to bed.
