Black Travelers: Cable-Light Vigil

921 wordsBridge Trilogy

"The cable-lights had been the bridge's signal-system for thirty years. They were also the Black Travelers' memory-system."

Hook

I am the eldest of the Black Travelers. My name is Cordis. I have lived on the bridge for twenty-three years, longer than most of the people who live on the bridge now, longer than most of the people who have ever lived on the bridge. I have seen the bridge change. I have seen the Lost-Norters come and go. I have seen the Armenians establish their kitchens. I have seen Chevette grow up. I have seen Skinner become who he is. I have seen the bridge become the bridge.

Tonight I am keeping the cable-light vigil. The cable-lights are the strings of small-LEDs that the Black Travelers strung along the bridge's suspension-cables thirty years ago. The cable-lights were a signal-system. The cable-lights were a way for the Travelers to communicate across the bridge without using radio or telephone. The cable-lights were a way for the Travelers to send messages, to warn of danger, to call for help.

Rising Action

The cable-lights are still working, most of them. The cable-lights have been repaired and replaced over the years. The cable-lights have been the Travelers' project, the Travelers' inheritance, the Travelers' memory.

The cable-lights have also been the Travelers' memory-system in a different way. The cable-lights have been the way the Travelers remember the people who have been on the bridge. Each cable-light has a name. Each cable-light has a person. When a Traveler dies, a new cable-light is strung in their name. When a Traveler is born on the bridge, a new cable-light is strung for them. The cable-lights are the Travelers' family-tree.

Tonight I am checking the cable-lights. I am walking along the bridge's third-tier, looking at each cable-light, making sure each cable-light is working, recording each cable-light's name in my head. The cable-lights that are not working, I will repair tomorrow. The cable-lights that have burned out, I will replace tomorrow.

The bridge is quiet tonight. The bridge is quiet most nights, for the people who live here. The city below is loud. The tourists are loud. The corporate-extraction-economy is loud. But the bridge is quiet, the way that water is quiet, the way that air is quiet, the way that a thing-that-has-always-been-there is quiet.

Midpoint Reversal

But tonight the bridge is not quiet in the way it usually is. Tonight the bridge has a sound that I have not heard before. The sound is coming from the second-tier west. The sound is a kind of humming. The sound is a kind of low-frequency-vibration that the bridge's structure is making, as if something inside the bridge's metal is trying to communicate.

I walk toward the sound. I walk carefully, because I am old, in bridge-years, which means I am maybe-fifty. I walk carefully because the bridge is dark in places, and I have learned to walk in the dark. I walk toward the sound because the sound is something new, and new things on the bridge need to be understood before they can be ignored.

The sound is coming from a piece of the bridge's structure that I have walked past a thousand times. The piece is a beam, a horizontal-support, a piece of metal that holds up the second-tier. The beam is humming. The beam is vibrating at a frequency that I can feel in my feet but cannot hear with my ears.

Climax

I put my hand on the beam. The beam is warm. The beam is warm in a way that the bridge's metal should not be warm. The bridge's metal is cold, especially at night, especially in the fog, especially in the wind off the Bay. The bridge's metal is the kind of metal that a person can lean against without feeling anything except the cold.

But this beam is warm. This beam is warm and humming. This beam is warm and humming and it is trying to communicate something. This beam is warm and humming and it is trying to communicate something that I do not understand.

I stand there for a long time, with my hand on the warm beam, listening to the humming. I do not understand what the humming means. I do not understand what the bridge is trying to tell me.

But I know that the bridge is trying to tell me something. The bridge has been trying to tell the Travelers things for thirty years. The bridge has been trying to tell the Travelers things through the cable-lights, through the saltline, through the slow-trade. The bridge has been trying to tell the Travelers things that the city below could not understand.

Resolution

I make a note in my head. I will tell the other Travelers tomorrow. I will tell the other Travelers that there is a beam on the second-tier west that is warm and humming. I will tell the other Travelers that the beam is trying to communicate something.

The Travelers will investigate. The Travelers will sit with the beam, the way we sit with the bridge, the way we sit with the cable-lights, the way we sit with everything on the bridge that is trying to communicate.

The bridge has always been trying to communicate. The bridge has always been trying to tell us things. The Travelers have always been listening.

"The bridge was a thing that was trying to communicate. The Travelers were the people who could hear. The cable-lights were the memory of all the things the bridge had said."

I walked back to my camp. The fog had thickened. The bridge-creak sounded louder in the night. The cable-lights glowed in the fog, each one a name, each one a memory, each one a piece of the bridge's history.

I sat down at the camp's edge and watched the cable-lights glow. The bridge was trying to communicate. The bridge was always trying to communicate.

The Travelers would listen. The Travelers would always listen.
