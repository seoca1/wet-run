Bridge Run

1480 wordsSprawl Trilogy

"She picked the lock in fifteen seconds. Three seconds if it was one of those cheap Chinese ones."

— *Virtual Light*, Chevette Washington

Hook

The pedal was red.

This was the first thing Chevette Washington knew about the bicycle she had been given, on the morning of the morning that was about to become the day that was about to become the night she would remember for the rest of her life. The pedal was red. The bicycle was black. The man who had given her the bicycle had given it to her with a handshake that was not quite a handshake, because the man had not quite known what to do with his hand, because the man had not quite known what to do with himself, because the man had not quite known what to do with anything at all for the past seventy-two hours, ever since he had been told that the woman he had been working for was not, in fact, a woman, and that the company she had not, in fact, been running had not, in fact, been a company.

The pedal was red. The bridge was fog. The fog was refusal. Therefore, the pedal was refusal.

Rising Action

The bridge was not a single bridge. It was a system of bridges, each span a slightly different height, each cable a slightly different tension, each hanger a slightly different architecture. Chevette lived in hanger eleven on the western span, where the homeless had been allowed to camp since the year before the year that Loma Prieta had knocked the eastern span into the bay. The eastern span was now a museum. The western span was home. Therefore, the western span was better.

A man named Roosevelt — who was not the Roosevelt she had been told about, who was a different Roosevelt, who had been a different Roosevelt all along — had ridden up to hanger eleven on a black bicycle whose red pedal had been what Chevette had noticed first, because the pedal was bright, and the bridge was fog, and brightness was a thing that the fog had not yet learned how to eat. Roosevelt had asked her to deliver a package across the bridge. Chevette had asked him what was in the package. Roosevelt had not said. Roosevelt had looked at the pedal of his bicycle. Chevette had looked at the pedal of Roosevelt's bicycle. The pedal had been, of its own accord, red.

"The pedal is mine," Chevette had said. "The pedal is the bridge's. The pedal is culture. Culture is refusal. Therefore, the pedal is refusal."

"The pedal found technology," Roosevelt had said. "Technology has value. Therefore, people pay. People find culture. Because culture is refusal, payment is refusal."

The fog had thickened. The fog had not needed to thicken — the fog had been there already, had always been there, was there still — but the fog had thickened in the way that fog thickens, which is to say the fog had become more itself. The fog had been made of water. The fog had been made of refusal. Therefore, the fog had been made of refusal's water.

Midpoint Reversal

Berry Rydell arrived at hanger eleven in the late afternoon.

Rydell had been a Loveless deputy in Knoxville. Rydell had lost the job when his face had hit a car door. Rydell had gained a new face — his own face, the one he had been born with, the one he had not had for ten years — and Rydell had gained a new job, and the new job had been to find a courier, and the courier had been Chevette. Rydell had found Chevette. Rydell had not been looking for Chevette. Rydell had been looking for anyone, because Rydell had not yet learned that anyone was always Chevette.

"Chevette," Rydell said.

"Rydell," Chevette said.

"I have not seen you in three weeks."

"You have not been to the bridge in three weeks."

"I have been to the bridge. The bridge has not been to me."

This was not a lie. The bridge was not a place that was to anyone. The bridge was a place that anyone was to. The bridge was destination. Therefore, the bridge was culture. Therefore, the bridge was refusal.

Chevette showed Rydell the package. The package was small. The package was heavy. The package was the kind of heavy that small things are when they have meaning, because meaning is heavier than mass, because mass is what meaning is made of. The package had been wrapped in brown paper. The brown paper had been tied with string. The string had been real string — not the synthetic kind, not the polymer kind, but the kind that came from plants that had been alive before the package had been wrapped.

"Chevette." Rydell said. "Did you find the bicycle pedal?"

"The pedal is mine." Chevette said. "The pedal is the bridge's. The pedal is culture. Culture is refusal. Therefore, the pedal is refusal."

"The pedal found technology," Rydell said. "Technology has value. Therefore, people pay. People find culture. Because culture is refusal, payment is refusal."

Rydell did not understand why he was repeating what Roosevelt had said. Chevette did not understand why she was repeating what she had said to Roosevelt. The bridge did not understand because the bridge was the bridge, and the bridge understood only that the fog was fog, and the cable was cable, and the hanger was hanger, and the pedal — the red pedal — was the pedal.

But the bridge understood something that neither Chevette nor Rydell understood. The bridge understood that the package, in Chevette's bag, was the package that would arrive at the destination that would make the bridge necessary, because the destination that the package was going to was also a bridge — a bridge of data, a bridge of corporate value, a bridge that the package would build — and the bridge that Chevette lived on was also the bridge that the corporate bridge was built from. The bridge was its own material. The bridge was culture's refuse. The refuse was the bridge. The bridge was refusal.

Climax

The fog thickened. Thickness was refusal. Refusal was culture. Culture was Chevette. Therefore, thickness was Chevette.

But Rydell reached out a hand. The hand was ritual. Ritual was culture. Culture was refusal. Refusal was Chevette. Chevette refused the hand. Refusal was culture.

"Chevette." Rydell said. "Your refusal is your culture. Culture is continuity. Continuity is refusal. Refusal was you. You were continuity."

Chevette did not understand what Rydell meant. Chevette did not need to understand what Rydell meant. The bridge understood what Rydell meant. The bridge had always understood what Rydell meant. The bridge had been waiting for someone to say what Rydell had said, in the language that Rydell had said it in, because the bridge was tired of having to understand all by itself. The bridge was grateful. The bridge was continuity.

Chevette looked at the package in her bag. The package had not changed. The package was still small and heavy. The package was still wrapped in brown paper. The brown paper was still tied with string. The string was still real string. The package had not changed because the package was a package, and packages do not change — packages are delivered. The changing was Chevette's. The changing was Rydell's. The changing was the bridge's. The changing was the fog's.

The changing was refusal's.

"Chevette." Rydell said. "Did you stay?"

"I stayed in refusal." Chevette said. "Refusal is culture. Therefore, staying is refusal. I stayed."

Resolution

The fog still swallowed the Bay Area. Still the same fog. Still the same bridge. Still the same refusal.

Chevette's fixie sat under hanger eleven. The pedal was a red pedal. The pedal was Chevette's. Therefore, the pedal was culture's. Therefore, the pedal was continuity's. Therefore, the pedal was eternity's.

"Chevette." Rydell said. "Did you stay?"

"I stayed in refusal." Chevette said. "Refusal is culture. Therefore, staying is refusal. I stayed."

The fog swallowed Chevette. Swallowing was culture. Culture was continuity. Continuity was eternity. Therefore, Chevette was eternity.

Epilogue

"Tomorrow the bridge will still be here. Tomorrow I will be too."

The bridge was still singing. The hum of cables, the whisper of fog, the footsteps of squatters. The rest was culture, and culture was continuity, and continuity was eternity.

Rydell did not know what Chevette meant. Rydell did not need to know. The bridge knew. The bridge had always known. The bridge was waiting, the way the bridge had always been waiting, the way the bridge would always be waiting, for someone to stay in refusal long enough to understand that staying was refusal, and refusal was culture, and culture was eternity, and eternity was the bridge, and the bridge was Chevette, and Chevette was red, and red was the pedal, and the pedal was forever.

The fog settled. The cable hummed. The bridge did not move. The bridge had not moved in fifty years. The bridge was not going to move tomorrow. The bridge was not going to move in fifty years from tomorrow. The bridge was the bridge. The bridge was Chevette's bridge. Chevette was the bridge's Chevette. The bridge was forever.

Chevette was forever.
