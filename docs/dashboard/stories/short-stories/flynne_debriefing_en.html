Post-Mission: Flynne in the Debriefing Room

Origin: Flynne's debriefing after the first major mission

1482 wordsPeripheral

The debriefing room had chairs around a table in the middle of which there was a pitcher of water that tasted faintly of metal. The pitcher was the kind of pitcher you would expect to find at a hotel conference in a city that was trying harder than it had to. The chairs were the kind of chairs you would expect to find at the same hotel conference. The room was on the third floor of a building that was not a hotel, in a city that was not a city I had been to, and I was the only person in the room who had not been in the room before.

The man across the table from me had been in the room before. He had been in the room many times. He had the look of a man who had been in this room so many times that the room had started to wear the particular dents and scratches that came from being in this room with him. He had a pen in his hand. He had a notepad in front of him. He had a cup of coffee that he had not touched at his right hand.

"Ms. Fisher."

"Wilf said I could be Flynne."

"Flynne. I'm sorry. I'm Detective Inspector Lev. I've been assigned to your debriefing."

"Detective Inspector."

"It's a title. It doesn't mean what it would have meant in your time. It means I am the person who asks you the questions after the mission so that the mission can be properly written down."

"Properly written down."

"Yes."

"In your notepad."

"In my notepad and in the official record and in the heads of the people who will need to know what happened so that the next mission can be properly arranged."

I looked at the pitcher of water. The water was clear. The pitcher was clear. The table underneath the pitcher was also clear. The chairs were not as clear.

"Flynne."

"What."

"Tell me what happened."

"What happened when."

"What happened during the mission."

"The mission was a thing that I did in a body that was not mine in a city that I have been in five times now, in which I walked to a place that I had been told to walk to and met a person that I had been told to meet and said things that I had been told to say and came back to the place that I had been told to come back to."

"That's the short version."

"That's the only version I have. The other version is the version that I experienced, which is the version that I am not going to write down in your notepad because the version I experienced is the version that I am going to keep."

Lev put his pen down. He looked at me with the look of a man who had been in this room with many operators of bodies that were not theirs and who had heard the same statement about keeping things from other operators of bodies that were not theirs.

"Flynne."

"Lev."

"You have to give me something for the official record."

"I gave you something for the official record. The mission was a thing that I did in a body that was not mine. I walked to a place. I met a person. I said things. I came back. That's the official record. That's what's going to be in your notepad."

"That's what's going to be in my notepad."

"Yes."

"What about the person."

"The person was a person. The person was a person that I had been told to meet. The person was not a person that I knew."

"Did the person say anything that I should know about."

"The person said the things that the person had been told to say. The person said the things that the person said because I said the things that I had been told to say and the person responded to the things that I said with the things that the person had been told to say."

"Flynne."

"Lev."

"That's a very even way of describing what happened."

"I am describing what happened in the even way. The even way is the way I have for this room. The other way is the way I have for the trailer. The even way goes in this room. The other way goes in the trailer."

Lev picked up his pen again. He wrote something in the notepad. The something was probably the even way.

"What did you keep."

"What did I keep what."

"You said you were going to keep the version you experienced. What did you keep of the version you experienced."

I looked at the pitcher of water. The water was still clear. The pitcher was still clear. The table was still clear. The chairs were not as clear, but the chairs were always going to be less clear than the pitcher because the chairs had been sat in by a lot of operators of bodies that were not theirs and the pitcher had not.

"I kept the part where the person I met was scared of me."

Lev wrote that down.

"I kept the part where the person I met was not scared of me because I was a person in a body that was not mine. The person I met was scared of me because the person I met could tell that I was a person in a body that was not mine, and the person I met knew what that meant."

Lev stopped writing.

"Lev."

"Flynne."

"The person I met knew what it meant. The person I met knew that I was a person from a past that the person I met had read about in school but had never expected to see in person. The person I met was scared of me the way you would be scared of a thing that had come out of a museum."

Lev wrote that down too.

"Flynne."

"Lev."

"What else did you keep."

"I kept the part where the person I met handed me something."

Lev looked up from the notepad.

"What did the person hand you."

"A piece of paper."

"What was on the piece of paper."

"I don't know."

"You don't know what was on the piece of paper."

"I don't know what was on the piece of paper because the piece of paper is in the trailer. The piece of paper is in the trailer because the piece of paper is a piece of paper that I brought back with me in the body that was mine. The piece of paper is a piece of paper that I am going to look at later, in the trailer, on the kitchen table, with Burton sitting across from me."

Lev put the pen down.

"Flynne."

"Lev."

"That piece of paper is evidence."

"That piece of paper is something that the person I met gave to me and I am going to look at later. That's what that piece of paper is."

"That's what that piece of paper is in this room. That's what that piece of paper is in the official record. That's what that piece of paper is in the notepad. The piece of paper is also evidence in a court that does not yet exist in your timeline."

"Lev."

"Flynne."

"I'm going to look at the piece of paper in the trailer first. Then I'm going to bring the piece of paper back to this room. Then you can put the piece of paper in the official record. Until then, the piece of paper is in the trailer."

Lev wrote.

"Flynne."

"Lev."

"We are going to write down that the piece of paper is in the trailer."

"We are going to write down that the piece of paper is in the trailer."

"And that you are going to bring the piece of paper back to this room."

"And that I am going to bring the piece of paper back to this room."

Lev finished writing. He closed the notepad. He picked up the coffee cup that he had not touched and emptied it into the pitcher of water that I had not touched, and the coffee went through the water in a brown plume that held its shape for a moment before dispersing.

"Thank you, Flynne."

"Thank you, Lev."

I stood up from the chair. The chair made a sound like the sound of a chair that has been sat in many times by operators of bodies that were not theirs. The sound was not a sad sound. The sound was a sound that had been earned.

I walked to the door.

The door opened.

The corridor outside was lit with a kind of light I had only ever seen in this office, in this city, in this future.

I walked into the light.
