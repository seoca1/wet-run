Yamazaki's Inventory

A Japanese Businessman Audits the Bridge

405 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The office was small. The office was on the bridge. The office had been set up by Otago Industries as a base of operations for Yamazaki's audit.

Yamazaki sat at a desk. Yamazaki had a small screen. Yamazaki had a small screen that showed the patterns.

The patterns were in the bridge. The patterns were in the data. The patterns were in the reconstruction logs.

Yamazaki was looking for anomalies.

Anomaly one: the lights in the bay that did not correspond to any vessel in the registry. Anomaly two: the camera placements that had been requested by a person who was not on any of the Otago's teams. Anomaly three: the contractor who had been hired to do a small job on the eastern span and who had, in the course of doing the job, replaced two of the load-bearing sensors without filing the proper paperwork.

Yamazaki wrote the anomalies down.

He thought about Laney. Laney was the man from Tokyo. Laney was the man who had been sent to find someone, and who had, in the course of finding the man, found a woman who was not a woman.

He thought about Colin. Colin was the man who had been hired to watch Laney. Colin had not been watching Laney. Colin had been watching the woman.

He thought about the bridge. The bridge was almost finished. The bridge had been almost finished for two years. The bridge was almost finished for a reason that no one in Otago's chain of command had been willing to explain to Yamazaki.

He wrote down another anomaly.

Anomaly four: the woman. The woman had been seen on the western span at 03:14. The woman had been seen on the western span at 03:14 by a contractor who had then, two days later, been reassigned.

He closed the screen.

He would, in the morning, send a report to Tokyo. The report would be read in Tokyo. The report would be read in Tokyo and then filed, and then not read again.

He did not mind.

Yamazaki had been auditing the bridge for three months. Yamazaki had been auditing the bridge for three months and he had learned to file reports that would not be read.

The bridge hummed.

The patterns, the woman, the sensors — all of it — hummed with a hum that was not on any of Yamazaki's charts.
