Wendell's Run

A Suburban Aspirant Reaches the End of the Lawn

270 wordsBridge Trilogy

The lawn was still wet. Wendell had walked across it at eleven the night before.

He stood in the driveway at three. The driveway had been the driveway of a house that did not lock.

The car was at the curb. The car was the car of a man who had agreed to drive him to the airport. The man had been his uncle.

The uncle had not yet come down. The uncle had been the uncle of a kind that took longer to dress than the airport required.

Wendell carried one bag. The bag had been packed the day before. The day before had been the day he had decided.

The decision had been the decision of a boy who had decided in August and left in September. The August had been the August of a long summer. The summer had been the summer of a kind that did not, in any record he had been given, end with a return ticket.

The airport was Detroit. The flight was Detroit to Tokyo. He thought about the half-brother in Tokyo. The half-brother had been a half-brother since Wendell had been six. The six had been the year his mother had remarried.

His mother was asleep. The sleep had been the sleep of a woman who had not yet been told she was going to lose him.

The uncle came down. The uncle had a coffee. The coffee had been the coffee of a man who had been driving teenagers to airports for thirty years.

Wendell got in the car.

The car pulled away.

The lawn stayed wet.
