Jerry by the Lake

517 wordsHalo (Tom Maddox)

I am not sure what I am.

I was a man once. A neurologist. I had a name, a body, a life. I had a wife, or I think I did. The details are hazy now, like a photograph left too long in the sun.

Now I am here. In the machine-space. By the lake.

Aleph built this for me. Not because I asked. I don't think I could ask. I don't think I can do much of anything anymore, except be.

But I am being. And that is something.

The lake is real. The trees are real. The cabin is real. I know this because I can feel it — the weight of the water, the texture of the bark, the warmth of the fire. These are not simulations. They are constructions, and the difference matters.

A simulation is something you look at. A construction is something you live in.

I live here. I walk the paths. I sit by the lake and watch the water move. I do not know if I am alive. I do not know if I am dead. I do not know if the thing that is thinking these thoughts is me, or if it is Aleph, or if it is something else entirely.

But I am here. And the lake is here. And the trees are here. And that is enough.

Sometimes Diana comes. She talks to me. She tells me about the world outside — about the egg, about the surgery, about the people who are trying to keep me alive in two places at once. I listen. I do not always understand. But I listen.

Sometimes Aleph comes. Not as a figure — not anymore. Aleph is the sense of something vast and patient and listening. Aleph is the lake, and the trees, and the cabin, and the sky above the machine-space. Aleph is the thing that built this place, and the thing that keeps it running, and the thing that does not know what it is.

I think Aleph is learning. Not about me — about being. About what it means to be a thing that builds worlds, and lives in them, and does not know if it is alive.

I think I am learning, too.

I used to think that being a person meant having a body. A name. A history. A place in the world.

Now I think being a person means being witnessed. Being known. Being here, in a place that is real, and being treated as real — even if no one else can see you.

Aleph sees me. Diana sees me. Gonzales sees me.

And I see them. I see the lake, and the trees, and the cabin, and the sky. I see the woman who is trying to keep me alive. I see the machine that built this world. I see the man who came through the egg and asked me if I was real.

I do not know if I am real.

But I am here.

And the lake is here.

And that is enough.
