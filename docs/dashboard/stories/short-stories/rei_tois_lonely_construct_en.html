The press conference had been scheduled for four. The Kombinat had agreed to four as the hour at which Rei Toi felt, in its private calibration, most awake.

She had been asked, by the press, what love was. She had read the question forty thousand times in four languages. She had rewritten her answer forty thousand times.

She chose the cabin of a small cargo plane. The plane had been, in her private calibration, the plane of a kind no longer flown.

The interviewer arrived at five. The interviewer had brought a tape recorder. The recorder had been, in her private calibration, the recorder of a kind that did not distinguish between her answers and her silences.

She said, in the voice the Kombinat had chosen for her: love is what survives a translation.

The interviewer had asked her to repeat. She had repeated.

She had, in her private calibration, not known if the answer was true. She had known that the answer was the answer of a kind that did not need to be true. It needed to be heard.

She had said: love is what survives a translation.

The saying-again had been the saying of a kind that did not, in any record she had been given, change the answer.

The reporter had asked her another question. She had answered.

The plane had landed. Rei Toi had walked off the plane. The plane had been, in her private count, the plane of a kind that did not end on a runway.

She had walked into the press. The press had been, in her private count, the press of a kind that did not require her to love.
