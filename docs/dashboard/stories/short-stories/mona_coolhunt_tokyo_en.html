Mona, Coolhunt: Tokyo

904 wordsBridge Trilogy

"Tokyo wasn't a city; it was the world's most expensive mood ring."

— Idoru, Mona's field-notes

도입 (Setup)

Mona had been in Tokyo for thirty-six hours when she realized she was being watched. The watching was professional. The watching had been Blue Ant's watching before the watching had been Blue Ant's. The watching had a name, in Blue Ant's terminology, that Mona had forgotten. The name's forgetting was Blue Ant's policy. The watching was what the watching was.

The watching did not bother Mona. Mona had been watched before. Mona had been watched in São Paulo. Mona had been watched in Lagos. Mona had been watched in Vancouver, where the watching had been the worst because Vancouver had been home.

Mona's assignment, in Tokyo, was a coolhunt for a client whose name was not the client's name but whose logo was the client's logo. The logo was a circle that Mona had been told was the client's name. The circle, in Tokyo's neon, was everywhere and nowhere. The circle was, in Tokyo's neon, the client's most successful product.

Mona's task was to determine whether the circle, in Tokyo, was the circle or whether the circle, in Tokyo, was something the circle had become in Tokyo. Mona's task was the coolhunt. Mona's coolhunt was the task.

전개 (Rising Action)

Mona's hotel was in Shibuya. The hotel's lobby had a screen on which advertisements cycled. Mona had watched the cycle, on the morning of her second day, for ninety minutes. The cycle showed the circle in seventeen contexts. The circle, in seventeen contexts, was the same circle. Mona had noted the sameness. The sameness was the coolhunt's first finding.

Mona left the hotel. Shibuya's pedestrian crossing was the world's largest pedestrian crossing. The crossing was a feature of Blue Ant's Tokyo presentations. The crossing was a feature of the client's circle. The crossing was, when Mona crossed it, a feature of Mona's field-notes.

The crossing's light changed. The crossing emptied. The crossing filled. The crossing emptied. Mona crossed. The crossing, Mona thought, was the circle.

Mona walked north, toward Harajuku. Harajuku's street fashion was, in the client's terms, the circle's proving-ground. Harajuku's street fashion was the coolhunt's primary data. Mona photographed. Mona noted. Mona purchased a small item of Harajuku street fashion. The small item was a sticker. The sticker was the client's circle. The sticker was, in Harajuku, ubiquitous.

The sticker's ubiquity was the coolhunt's second finding.

절정 (Climax)

In Harajuku, Mona entered a shop. The shop sold, among other things, a small device that Mona had been told was popular among Harajuku's street-fashion cohort. The device was a phone. The phone was not, Mona knew, a phone. The phone was the phone's phone. The phone was the world's most popular phone. The phone was, in Harajuku, ubiquitous.

Mona asked the shop's clerk about the phone. The clerk spoke Japanese. The clerk did not speak English. Mona spoke Japanese. Mona did not speak, well, Japanese. The clerk and Mona negotiated. The negotiation produced a translation. The translation was incomplete. The translation was enough.

The phone, the clerk explained, was not popular because of its features. The phone was popular because of its case. The phone's case was, the clerk explained, replaceable. The phone's case was, the clerk explained, a status-display. The phone was a status-display that made calls. The status-display was, the clerk explained, the phone.

Mona asked the clerk about the client's circle. The clerk said: the circle is not a status-display. The circle is not a phone. The circle is the circle. The circle, the clerk said, is what we have decided the circle is.

Mona purchased a phone. Mona purchased, from the same shop, a phone-case. The phone-case was the client's circle. The phone-case was, the clerk explained, the most popular phone-case in Harajuku.

The phone-case's popularity was the coolhunt's third finding.

결말 (Resolution)

Mona returned to the hotel. Mona wrote the field-notes. The field-notes were, in their entirety, three findings: the circle is the circle, the circle is the phone, the circle is the phone-case.

The three findings were the coolhunt. The coolhunt was the assignment. The assignment was, in Blue Ant's terms, the client. The client was, in Tokyo's terms, the circle.

Mona sent the field-notes to Blue Ant. Blue Ant acknowledged. Blue Ant thanked Mona. Blue Ant paid Mona. Blue Ant asked Mona to remain in Tokyo for one more day. Blue Ant asked Mona to determine what the circle would be in six months. Blue Ant told Mona that Blue Ant would send a young colleague to assist.

The young colleague's name, Blue Ant told Mona, was Cayce Pollard.

Mona, in her hotel room, did not react. Mona, in her hotel room, made a note. The note said: Cayce Pollard.

The note, Mona knew, was the next coolhunt. The note was, in Blue Ant's terms, the future.
