Rydell: Black Alice Encounter

861 wordsBridge Trilogy

Hook

"She was the bridge's immune system, Rydell realized. She tracked the bridge's body-modifications the way a doctor tracks infections."

Rydell met Black Alice on the third-tier of the bridge's east-section, in the part of the structure that nobody went to after dark because the deck-planking was too far gone. She was sitting cross-legged on a beam that didn't have a name, her back against a vertical-support, her face half-lit by the bridge's distant-arc-lights.

She was younger than he'd expected. Mid-twenties. Thin. Wearing what looked like a standard-issue black-T over what was probably a standard-issue black-cargo-pants, and a pair of boots that had been re-soled so many times the original-brand was just a memory.

"Mister Rydell," she said. Not a question.

"How do you know my name?"

"I know everyone's name. It's my job."

Rising Action

She gestured for him to sit. He sat on the beam across from her, his feet dangling over a hundred-and-fifty-foot drop that he tried not to think about. Black Alice didn't seem to notice the drop. She probably hadn't noticed it in years.

"You're new," she said. "On the bridge. I can tell because you keep looking down."

"Is that bad?"

"It's just new. The down-look fades. Eventually you stop seeing the drop and you start seeing the structure. The structure is what's interesting. The drop is just gravity doing its job."

She reached into her cargo-pants and pulled out a small-device, the size of a cigarette-case, with a single red LED on the side. She held it up between them.

"This is what I do," she said. "I track the bridge's body-modifications. New-tattoos. New-cyberware. New-scars that aren't from falls. The bridge has a body, Mister Rydell, and the body changes, and I track the changes."

"Why?"

Midpoint Reversal

"Because somebody has to. The bridge has its own immune-system, its own way of telling who belongs and who doesn't. I am part of that system."

She clicked the device. The red LED blinked. A small-text-stream appeared on a tiny-screen on the device's face, too small for Rydell to read.

"You've been here eleven days," she said. "You've got a new-cut on your left-hand, a small-one, you probably don't remember getting it. You've put on three pounds. You've been sleeping better. Your cortisol is down."

"How do you know my cortisol?"

"I don't, exactly. But you look less-stressed than you did when you got here. That's a cortisol-sign."

Rydell looked at her. The bridge-creak sounded louder in the silence. The wind off the Bay was getting colder. Up here, on this beam, in the dark, Black Alice was the only other living thing within fifty-feet.

"You're IntenSecure," she said. "Or you were. Lovelock's crew."

Climax

"Yeah."

"He was a good man. He died doing something stupid because somebody paid him to do something stupid. That's the corporate-math. He knew it. You knew it. He did it anyway."

"He did it because they said there was no risk."

"There's always risk. The corporate-math lies about risk. Lovelock knew that too. He just didn't let himself believe it."

Rydell thought about this. Black Alice put her device away and looked at him with the patient-attention of someone who had heard this story before, in different parking-lots, in different bridges, in different cities.

"You'll be okay," she said. "The bridge takes care of people like you. People who lost something. People who don't have anywhere else. People who are willing to be in-between."

"How do you know?"

Resolution

"Because the bridge took care of me. And I lost something. And I didn't have anywhere else. And I was willing."

She stood up. She was shorter than he'd expected, and lighter. He couldn't imagine her surviving a Bay-winter on the bridge, but she had, for years by the look of her.

"Stay out of the south-tier after midnight," she said. "The Armenian-vendors are doing a deal with some people from down the hill, and the deals are tense this week. I'll let you know when it's safe."

"Thanks."

"Don't thank me. Just stay alive. The bridge needs more people like you."

She walked away on the beam, balance-perfect, her black-clothes disappearing into the bridge's structural-dark. Rydell sat there for another minute, looking at the drop, trying not to look at the drop, then looking at the structure instead.

The structure was interesting. Black Alice was right about that.

"Some people are made for the bridge. Some people are made by the bridge. Rydell suspected he was the second kind."
