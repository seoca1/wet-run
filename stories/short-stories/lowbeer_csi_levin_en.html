도입 (Setup)

The call came at 03:14, on a Tuesday that was not a Tuesday but had the weather of a Tuesday. Lowbeer had been in her office in the Embarcadero tower that the city fathers had decided, after the quake, to keep standing. The tower's lobby had been rebuilt twice. The office Lowbeer kept had not been rebuilt. Lowbeer had resisted the rebuild, on grounds that Lowbeer had not shared with anyone.

The caller was a number Lowbeer had not seen in eighteen months. Lowbeer recognized the number. Lowbeer let the call ring once. Lowbeer answered.

"Levin," the caller said. "The body is in the bridge."

"Which bridge," Lowbeer said.

"The bridge," the caller said.

Lowbeer set the receiver in its cradle. Lowbeer looked at the wall, on which hung, in a frame, a postcard from a place Lowbeer had visited once and never intended to revisit. The postcard showed a beach. The beach was empty. Lowbeer had kept the postcard because the beach was empty.

전개 (Rising Action)

Levin's body was found under the bridge's eastern approach, in a hollow that the homeless had named. The hollow had been called many things, by many homeless, in the time Lowbeer had been working. The hollow was not a place where bodies were usually found. The hollow was a place where bodies were usually avoided.

Lowbeer examined the body through the lens Lowbeer had brought. The lens was Lowbeer's own design. Lowbeer had not patented the lens. The lens cost what the lens cost. The body showed what bodies showed.

The bridge's underside was a settlement now. Lowbeer had been told that the settlement was a courier's community. Lowbeer had been told that the courier's name was Chevette. Lowbeer had not yet met Chevette. Lowbeer had been told that meeting Chevette was unavoidable.

Levin had been a courier. Lowbeer had known Levin's courier license for three years. Levin had worked the Bay Bridge's data-key routes. Levin had been, Lowbeer believed, trustworthy. Levin's trustworthiness had been Levin's only asset. Levin's trustworthiness had been, until tonight, sufficient.

Lowbeer stood. Lowbeer's knees announced themselves. Lowbeer ignored them. Lowbeer walked, in the dark, the bridge's underside. Lowbeer counted seventeen settlements that Lowbeer had not known about. Lowbeer counted seven settlements that Lowbeer had known about and had lost. Lowbeer counted, in the dark, the bridge's silence. The silence was what the silence always was.

절정 (Climax)

The weapon was not at the scene. The weapon, Lowbeer suspected, had been at the scene and had been removed. The weapon's removal had been, Lowbeer believed, the point. The removal was a message. Lowbeer read messages for a living. Lowbeer had read this one before.

The message was: Lowbeer will know.

Lowbeer knew.

Lowbeer knew that the message was about a data-key that Levin had been carrying for someone Lowbeer had been watching for eighteen months. Lowbeer had known, eighteen months ago, that the someone would make a move. Lowbeer had not known the move would be Levin's death. Lowbeer had assumed the move would be more typical. The move had been atypical. The atypicality was the message within the message.

Lowbeer stood at the bridge's edge, looking down at the water. The water had been post-quake water, then had been reconstruction-water, and was now what the water was. Lowbeer looked at the water for a long time.

Levin's last data-key, Lowbeer had been told, was missing. The data-key had been the courier's responsibility. The data-key's destination had been a name Lowbeer did not yet recognize. The name, Lowbeer had been told, was Laney's. Laney was a node-artist. Lowbeer had watched Laney. Lowbeer had not known Laney was the data-key's destination.

Lowbeer knew now.

결말 (Resolution)

Lowbeer walked back to the car. Lowbeer's driver was waiting. Lowbeer did not enter the car immediately. Lowbeer looked, for a moment, at the bridge's underside, at the settlement that the homeless had named. The settlement held, in Lowbeer's sight, a small fire. The small fire was, in Lowbeer's sight, the only honest thing Lowbeer had seen in two days.

Lowbeer entered the car. The car drove. Lowbeer did not look at the bridge again.

In the Embarcadero tower, Lowbeer sat at the desk Lowbeer had resisted rebuilding. Lowbeer opened the file Lowbeer kept for cases that would never close. Lowbeer added Levin's name. Lowbeer added the bridge. Lowbeer added the date. Lowbeer closed the file.

The file, Lowbeer knew, was a file that would be opened again. The file was always opened again.

Lowbeer looked at the postcard on the wall. The postcard's beach was still empty. Lowbeer had kept the postcard because the beach was empty. The beach was still empty. The bridge had not been empty. The bridge had been full of settlements, and small fires, and a body that would never be found.

Lowbeer turned off the office light. Lowbeer left.
