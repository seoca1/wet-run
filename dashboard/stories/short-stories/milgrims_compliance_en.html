Milgrim's Compliance

1519 wordsBlue Ant Trilogy

"The protocol rewired the want. The want stopped wanting. The want left a gap where the habit was."

— Spook Country, Milgrim's internal monologue

Introduction

The hotel room in Vancouver was the fifteenth Milgrim had stayed in that year. The room was identical to all the others. The room was the protocol's compliance. The protocol was Bigend's compliance. Bigend was Milgrim's compliance. Milgrim was the protocol.

The pill was blue. The pill was white. The pill was yellow. The pill was the protocol's color sequence. The protocol rewired the want. The want stopped wanting. The want left a gap where the habit was. The habit had been substance. The substance had been escape. The escape had been death. The death had been waiting.

Now the death was waiting differently. The death was waiting in the package. The package was due at noon. The package was a Toshiba laptop, a Hudson sunglasses case, a vintage Nokia 3310, and three external hard drives. The package was the reason Milgrim was desperate. The desperation was the protocol's. The desperation was the gap.

Milgrim's hands didn't shake. Milgrim's hands hadn't shaken in eleven months. The shaking had been the want. The want had been the substance. The substance had been the brown-blue-pink rush that came in vials stamped with suns and stars, sold by men who lived in the marginal gaps between Moscow's financial regulation and Berlin's underground railroad. The addiction was the original substance. The addiction had been the gap's original occupant.

Bigend had found him in rehab in Zürich. Bigend had paid for the protocol. Bigend had given Milgrim a choice: continue the way he was, or take the protocol and work for Blue Ant. Milgrim had taken the protocol. Therefore, therefore, therefore — Milgrim was here. Milgrim was waiting.

Rising Action

The package arrived at 9:00. It contained: a Toshiba laptop, a Hudson sunglasses case, a vintage Nokia 3310, and three external hard drives. Each item was delivered by a different courier. Each courier was brandless. Each courier spoke no English.

The laptop contained: forty-seven documents. The documents were: ops briefs. The ops briefs were: the Brown operation's final itinerary. The itinerary was: Busan → Vancouver → Rotterdam → Vancouver. The itinerary was the package.

The sunglasses contained: a micro-camera, a bluetooth transmitter, and a small flag bearing the Brazilian tricolor. The flag was for identification. The micro-camera was for surveillance. The transmission was for Cayce.

The phone contained: a single contact. The contact was named "Panther." The contact was: Cayce Pollard. The phone was Cayce's emergency line. The line was for emergencies. The emergencies were: the package. The package was: the question. The question was: what Bigend wanted.

The hard drives contained: 412 hours of Tito's father's footage. 47 hours of the Brown operation's internal communications. 12 hours of Bigend's own interceptions. The drives were: Tito's inheritance. Brown's confession. Bigend's archive. The archive was the question.

Milgrim didn't open the drives. Milgrim wasn't authorized. The drives were: Cayce's assignment. Cayce would audit. Cayce would interpret. The interpretation was what Bigend paid for. The interpretation was the protocol's work. Therefore Milgrim was: the courier. Milgrim was: the witness. The addiction to compliance was the new substance.

The protocol kicked in. Milgrim's pulse slowed. Milgrim's jaw unclenched. The clock on the wall said 11:47. The clock said noon was approaching. The noon was: the deadline. The deadline was: Cayce's audit. The audit was the new dose of the compliance addiction.

Inciting

11:48. The hotel phone rang. It wasn't Bigend. It wasn't Cayce. It was Windgate — the Brown operation's last survivor, the man whose eyes said he'd seen too much and remembered none of it. Windgate was coming. Windgate had escaped something. Windgate was desperate. Windgate needed the package to never reach Cayce.

Milgrim's hand didn't shake. Milgrim's hand shivered — a new sensation. The protocol hadn't rewired shiver. The protocol had left shiver intact because shiver wasn't the substance. Shiver was fear. The protocol had taken the fear. The protocol had taken the wrong fear.

"Milgrim." Windgate's voice on the hotel line. "Cayce is compromised. Bigend knows. Brown told them everything in 2008. The package is the trap. Cayce is walking into the trap because Cayce doesn't know she's already inside it."

Milgrim's jaw stayed unclenched. The protocol kept working.

"Cayce walks in at noon. The drives contain vectors. The vectors activate on Cayce's laptop. The vectors open her comms to Bigend's archive. The archive is the trap. The trap is the protocol extended."

Therefore, therefore, therefore.

"Then we don't give Cayce the drives."

"Then Cayce dies. The protocol has already been extended to survive the death. Milgrim, you've been the protocol for eleven months. The protocol isn't yours. The protocol is Bigend's. The protocol is the trap."

Climax

The hotel door opened at 11:52. Cayce Pollard walked in, her messenger bag over her shoulder, her sunglasses still on. Behind her: Tito, the Cuban's camera eye catching the late-afternoon Vancouver light. Behind Tito: Windgate — who had been Cayce's escort all along, who had betrayed the protocol for nothing, who had betrayed nothing for everything.

The trap was activated. Milgrim knew it. The protocol knew it. The addiction to compliance kept the trap running.

"The package." Cayce set her bag on the desk. "Bigend wants it opened. Bigend wants to know what's in the drives."

Milgrim's hands didn't shake. Milgrim's hands hadn't shaken in eleven months. The protocol had rewired the want. The want had left a gap. The gap was where the habit used to be. The gap was where the substance used to be. The gap was where the human used to be. The gap was where the betrayal now sat. The addiction to compliance was the gap's new shape.

"The drives are: Tito's father's footage. Brown's communications. Bigend's archive." Milgrim's voice was the protocol's voice — flat, emotionless, almost robotic. "The drives are: vectors. The vectors are: the trap. The trap is: the protocol extended."

Cayce's hand stopped on the Toshiba. Her sunglasses came off. Her eyes — eyes that had been brand-immune since 1989 — went wide.

"The trap is you, Milgrim."

"The trap is the gap. The gap is the human. The human is what we lost to the protocol. The protocol won. The protocol is the trap."

"Then we destroy the protocol."

"The protocol is in my blood. The protocol is in my neurons. The protocol is the gap. We don't destroy the gap."

"We destroy Bigend."

"We can't. Bigend is the protocol. The protocol is the trap. The trap is the gap. The gap is the human. Therefore the human is what we destroy."

Resolution

Cayce set down the Toshiba. She set down the drives. She set down the protocol that had been Milgrim's life for eleven months. She set them down on the hotel desk and walked to the window and looked out at the Vancouver harbor.

"We don't destroy the protocol. We leave it."

"The protocol doesn't let us leave."

"The protocol doesn't want." Cayce's voice was small. The smallest Cayce had ever been. "The protocol wants nothing. The protocol is the gap. The gap is the place where the want used to be. The gap is the place where we can build something else."

Milgrim's hand shivered. The protocol kept working. The protocol kept the hand able to work. The protocol kept the hand unable to want.

"Build what?"

"A witness." Cayce turned. "Not the gap. Not the protocol. You. Milgrim. The human that the gap replaced. The human is still there. The human is in the gap. The gap has been the human all along."

The clock read 13:04. The deadline had passed. The protocol had not. The addiction to compliance kept the protocol breathing.

Milgrim looked at his hands. His hands didn't shake. His hands shivered. The shiver was the witness. The witness was the human. The human was back.

The Vancouver harbor was still visible. The mountain was still invisible. The protocol was still in his blood. The addiction to compliance was still in his blood.

But the gap — the gap had become the witness. The witness had become the human. The human had become Milgrim. The addiction had lost its grip on the gap. The gap had become the door.

The protocol had won. The protocol had lost.

The trap had become a door. The addiction had become the witness.
