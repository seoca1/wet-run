Cultural Pattern: Bigend at Lunch

Midpoint: The Recruitment

4665 wordsBlue Ant Trilogy

"The future is not Google. The future is not even Apple. The future is the thing that arrives on a Tuesday afternoon in a brown paper-folder."

— Bigend, internal memo, 2002

Phase 1: Opening Hook — The Lunch

The lunch was at Le Gavroche. The lunch was at one. The lunch was, Bigend realised, not a lunch. The lunch was a recruitment. The recruitment was, Bigend realised, not a recruitment. The recruitment was, Bigend realised, a kind of pattern-recognition exercise. The pattern-recognition exercise was, Bigend realised, not what Blue Ant called a pattern-recognition exercise. The Blue Ant term for it was cultural-pattern-mapping.

Bigend arrived at twelve-forty. The twelve-forty was deliberate. The twelve-forty was, Bigend realised, the kind of arrival-time that said I am early enough to be observed, late enough not to be eager. The twelve-forty was a position. The position was a brand.

The maitre d' greeted him. The maitre d' was named Bernard. The maitre d' was wearing a black waistcoat. The black waistcoat was, Bigend realised, the kind of waistcoat that the maitre d' had been wearing for twenty-three years. The twenty-three-years was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that Le Gavroche had been cultivating for thirty-eight years. The thirty-eight-years was the substrate.

"Mr. Bigend," Bernard said. "Your party has not yet arrived."

"Good," Bigend said.

Bigend followed Bernard to the table. The table was at the back of the dining-room. The back of the dining-room was, Bigend realised, the kind of back-of-the-dining-room that Le Gavroche reserved for clients who did not want to be seen. The clients who did not want to be seen were, Bigend realised, the kind of clients that Le Gavroche cultivated. The cultivated-clients were the substrate.

Bigend sat down. The chair was a brass-and-thonet. The brass-and-thonet was, Bigend realised, the kind of chair that Le Gavroche had been using for thirty-eight years. The thirty-eight-years was the substrate.

The dining-room was small. The dining-room had twelve tables. The twelve-tables were, Bigend realised, the kind of twelve-tables that a small-restaurant had. The small-restaurant was the substrate.

He ordered a Perrier. The Perrier was, Bigend realised, the kind of Perrier that he always ordered. The always-ordered was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that he had been cultivating for thirty years. The thirty-years was, Bigend realised, the kind of thirty-years that said I have a system.

The client arrived at one. The client was a man in a dark suit whose name was Aldous. The client's name was, Bigend realised, the kind of name that the British upper-middle-class cultivated. The cultivated-name was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that the client had been born into.

Aldous sat down across from Bigend. Aldous placed a black-leather briefcase on the table. The black-leather briefcase was, Bigend realised, the kind of black-leather briefcase that the British upper-middle-class carried. The carried-briefcase was the substrate.

"Thanks for coming," Aldous said.

"Thanks for the lunch," Bigend said.

"How was your morning?" Aldous asked.

"Busy," Bigend said. Bigend did not elaborate. Elaborating on mornings was a corporate-ritual that Bigend preferred to abbreviate, because Bigend had learned, in thirty years of corporate-lunches, that the corporate-ritual of morning-recap was a kind of pattern-recognition failure. The pattern-recognition failure was, Bigend realised, the kind of pattern-recognition failure that British upper-middle-class clients exhibited at lunch.

Aldous opened the briefcase. The briefcase contained a single sheet of paper. The single-sheet-of-paper contained a single paragraph. The single-paragraph was, Bigend realised, the kind of single-paragraph that a British upper-middle-class client wrote when he wanted to brief a marketing-consultant. The written-brief was the substrate.

"We're losing ground," Aldous said. "The category is consolidating. We need to re-position."

"Re-position," Bigend repeated.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"Re-position," Bigend repeated, "is a word that means I have not yet decided what the brand is."

Aldous paused.

"Possibly," Aldous said.

"Re-position is, in my experience, a word that means I have decided what the brand is, but I have not decided whether to admit it," Bigend said. "The brand is, in either case, already in the substrate. The substrate is, in either case, what the brand is not. The brand is, in either case, the missing-thing in the substrate. The missing-thing is, in either case, what the marketing-consultant is being hired to identify."

Aldous paused.

"Identify," Aldous repeated.

"Yes," Bigend said.

The waiter arrived. The waiter was named Henri. Henri was wearing a black waistcoat. The black waistcoat was, Bigend realised, the same black waistcoat that Bernard was wearing. The same-black-waistcoat was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that Le Gavroche cultivated.

"May I take your order, sir?" Henri asked.

Aldous ordered the crab-cake. The crab-cake was, Bigend realised, the kind of crab-cake that Le Gavroche served as a starter. The crab-cake was the substrate.

Bigend ordered the quail. The quail was, Bigend realised, the kind of quail that he always ordered. The always-ordered was the substrate.

Henri left. The leaving was, Bigend realised, the kind of leaving that a French waiter did. The French-waiter was the substrate.

"The category," Bigend said.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"The category is consolidating," Bigend said. "The consolidating is, in my experience, the kind of consolidating that happens when the substrate has saturated. The saturated-substrate is, in my experience, the kind of saturated-substrate that produces consolidation. The consolidation is, in my experience, what the brand-strategy is for. The brand-strategy is, in my experience, the kind of brand-strategy that the British upper-middle-class client writes when he has not yet decided whether to admit what the brand is."

Aldous looked at the table.

"What is the brand?" Aldous asked.

"That," Bigend said, "is the wrong question."

"The wrong question," Aldous repeated.

"The wrong question is what is the brand," Bigend said. "The right question is what is the substrate. The right question is what is the saturated-substrate. The right question is what is the missing-thing in the saturated-substrate. The right question is, in my experience, the only question that a marketing-consultant can answer. The marketing-consultant is, in my experience, the kind of marketing-consultant that the British upper-middle-class client hires when he has decided that he does not want to answer the question himself. The not-wanting-to-answer is the substrate. The substrate is, in my experience, the kind of substrate that the British upper-middle-class client cultivates."

Aldous paused.

"I cultivate," Aldous said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The cultivation," Aldous said, "is not the brand."

"No," Bigend said.

"The cultivation," Aldous said, "is the substrate."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The substrate," Aldous said, "is not the brand."

"No," Bigend said.

"The brand," Aldous said, "is the missing-thing in the substrate."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The missing-thing," Aldous said, "is what you are being hired to identify."

"Yes," Bigend said.

Henri returned with the crab-cake and the quail. The crab-cake was, Bigend realised, the kind of crab-cake that Le Gavroche served as a starter. The quail was, Bigend realised, the kind of quail that he always ordered. The always-ordered was the substrate.

Bigend ate the quail. The quail was good. The good was the substrate.

Aldous ate the crab-cake. The crab-cake was good. The good was the substrate.

Phase 2: Rising Action — The Pattern

Bigend sat back in the brass-and-thonet. The brass-and-thonet was, Bigend realised, the kind of chair that Le Gavroche had been using for thirty-eight years. The thirty-eight-years was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that he had been reading for thirty years.

"Aldous," Bigend said.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"You are not the brand," Bigend said.

"No," Aldous said.

"You are not the substrate," Bigend said.

"No," Aldous said.

"You are the missing-thing in the substrate," Bigend said.

Aldous paused.

"I am the missing-thing," Aldous said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"I am the missing-thing in the substrate," Aldous said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The substrate is what you are," Aldous said.

"No," Bigend said. "The substrate is what is around you. The substrate is what the brand-strategy is trying to replace. The substrate is what the brand-strategy is trying to identify. The substrate is what the brand-strategy is trying to be. The substrate is, however, not the brand. The brand is what is not the substrate. The brand is what is missing from the substrate. The brand is, in your case, you."

Aldous looked at the table.

"Me," Aldous said.

"You," Bigend said. "Specifically. Your accent. Your briefcase. Your crab-cake. Your waistcoat. Your chair. Your table. Your name. Your cultivation. Your thirty-eight-years of Le Gavroche. Your thirty-eight-years of brass-and-thonet. Your thirty-eight-years of black waistcoat. Your thirty-eight-years of Henri and Bernard. Your thirty-eight-years of the back-of-the-dining-room. Your thirty-eight-years of not-wanting-to-be-seen."

Aldous paused.

"Not-wanting-to-be-seen," Aldous repeated.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"Not-wanting-to-be-seen," Aldous said, "is the brand."

"No," Bigend said. "Not-wanting-to-be-seen is the substrate. The substrate is what the brand is not. The brand is what is not the substrate. The brand is what is missing from the substrate. The brand is, in your case, the missing-thing."

Aldous looked at the table.

"The missing-thing," Aldous said.

"The missing-thing," Bigend said.

Henri returned. Henri was carrying a single bottle of wine. The wine was a Burgundy. The Burgundy was, Bigend realised, the kind of Burgundy that Le Gavroche served as a house-wine. The house-wine was the substrate.

Henri poured the Burgundy. The Burgundy was, Bigend realised, the colour of a particular shade of red. The particular-shade was the substrate.

Bigend drank the Burgundy. The Burgundy was good. The good was the substrate.

Aldous drank the Burgundy. The Burgundy was good. The good was the substrate.

"Aldous," Bigend said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The brand," Bigend said, "is not what you want to be. The brand is what is missing from what you want to be. The brand is what is missing from your not-wanting-to-be-seen. The brand is what is missing from your cultivation. The brand is what is missing from your thirty-eight-years of Le Gavroche. The brand is what is missing from your briefcase. The brand is what is missing from your name. The brand is what is missing from your accent. The brand is, in short, what is missing from you."

Aldous paused.

"What is missing from me," Aldous said.

"What is missing from you," Bigend said.

"What is missing from me," Aldous said.

"What is missing from you," Bigend said.

Aldous looked at the table.

"The brand," Aldous said, "is what is missing from me."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The brand," Aldous said, "is what I have been cultivating."

"No," Bigend said. "The brand is what you have not been cultivating. The brand is what you have been missing. The brand is what is missing from what you have been cultivating. The brand is what you have not been cultivating because you have not known what to cultivate. The brand is what is missing from your knowing what to cultivate."

Aldous looked at the table.

"I have not known," Aldous said.

"You have not known," Bigend said.

"The brand," Aldous said, "is what I have not known."

"No," Bigend said. "The brand is what you have not known about. The brand is what you have not known you were missing. The brand is what you have not known you were not cultivating. The brand is what you have not known you were cultivating by not cultivating. The brand is, in short, the thing that your not-cultivating has been cultivating for you. The brand is the side-effect. The side-effect is, in my experience, the only thing that a brand-strategy can produce. The brand-strategy is, in my experience, the kind of brand-strategy that the British upper-middle-class client writes when he wants to produce a side-effect. The side-effect is, in my experience, what the marketing-consultant is being hired to identify."

Aldous paused.

"Identify," Aldous repeated.

"Yes," Bigend said.

Henri returned with the main course. The main course was a roast. The roast was, Bigend realised, the kind of roast that Le Gavroche served as a main course. The roast was the substrate.

Bigend ate the roast. The roast was good. The good was the substrate.

Aldous ate the roast. The roast was good. The good was the substrate.

The understanding came not as a revelation but as a kind of reorganisation. The reorganisation was, Bigend realised, the kind of reorganisation that Blue Ant had been producing for clients for thirty years. The reorganisation was, Bigend realised, the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the missing-thing.

"Aldous," Bigend said.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"You are not the brand," Bigend said.

"No," Aldous said.

"You are not the substrate," Bigend said.

"No," Aldous said.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"The missing-thing," Bigend said, "is what you are missing. The missing-thing is what you have not been cultivating. The missing-thing is what your not-cultivating has been cultivating for you. The missing-thing is, in my experience, what the brand-strategy is for. The brand-strategy is, in my experience, the kind of brand-strategy that the British upper-middle-class client writes when he wants to identify the missing-thing. The missing-thing is, in my experience, what the marketing-consultant is being hired to identify."

The brand is, in your case, what is missing from the identification. The brand is, in your case, what is missing from my identification. "Yes," Bigend said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

Henri returned. Henri was carrying a single dessert. The dessert was a chocolate-mousse. The chocolate-mousse was, Bigend realised, the kind of chocolate-mousse that Le Gavroche served as a dessert. The chocolate-mousse was the substrate.

Bigend ate the chocolate-mousse. The chocolate-mousse was good. The good was the substrate.

Aldous ate the chocolate-mousse. The chocolate-mousse was good. The good was the substrate.

"Aldous," Bigend said.

"Yes," Aldous said.

"Please," Aldous said.

"Who," Bigend said, "is the client?"

Aldous paused.

"I am the client," Aldous said.

"No," Bigend said. "You are not the client. You are the substrate. The substrate is not the client. The client is the missing-thing in the substrate. The missing-thing is, in your case, the person you are reporting to. The person you are reporting to is the client. The person you are reporting to is the missing-thing in your not-cultivating. The person you are reporting to is the missing-thing in your thirty-eight-years of Le Gavroche. The person you are reporting to is, in short, the brand."

Aldous paused.

"The brand," Aldous said, "is the person I am reporting to."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The person I am reporting to," Aldous said, "is the missing-thing in my thirty-eight-years of Le Gavroche."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The missing-thing," Aldous said, "is what I have not been cultivating."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The missing-thing," Aldous said, "is what my not-cultivating has been cultivating for me."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The missing-thing," Aldous said, "is the side-effect."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The side-effect," Aldous said, "is what you are being hired to identify."

"Yes," Bigend said.

Henri returned with the bill. The bill was, Bigend realised, the kind of bill that Le Gavroche produced. The bill was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that Le Gavroche had been cultivating for thirty-eight years. The thirty-eight-years was the substrate.

Bigend took the bill. Bigend paid the bill. Bigend added a thirty-percent tip. The thirty-percent tip was, Bigend realised, the kind of tip that a French waiter expected. The French-waiter was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that Le Gavroche cultivated.

Aldous stood up. Aldous took his briefcase. Aldous walked to the door. Aldous did not say goodbye. Aldous did not say thank-you. Aldous did not, Bigend realised, say anything. The not-saying was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that the British upper-middle-class client cultivated.

Bigend stood up. Bigend walked to the door. Bigend did not say goodbye. Bigend did not say thank-you. Bigend did not, Bigend realised, say anything. The not-saying was the substrate. The substrate was, Bigend realised, the kind of substrate that he had been cultivating for thirty years. The thirty-years was, Bigend realised, the kind of thirty-years that said I have a system.

The number was Cayce Pollard's.

Phase 4: Climax — The Call

The phone rang four times. The four-times was, Bigend realised, the kind of four-times that Cayce Pollard allowed. The Cayce-Pollard-allowed was the substrate.

"Hello," Cayce said.

"Cayce," Bigend said.

"Yes," Cayce said.

"I am standing on a street in London," Bigend said. "I have just had lunch with a client. The client was a man named Aldous. The man-named-Aldous was, in my experience, the kind of British upper-middle-class client that Blue Ant has been cultivating for thirty years. The British-upper-middle-class-client was the substrate. The substrate was, in my experience, what the brand-strategy is for. The brand-strategy was, in my experience, what the client was not. The client was, in my experience, the missing-thing in the substrate. The missing-thing was, in my experience, what the marketing-consultant is being hired to identify."

Cayce paused.

"Bigend," Cayce said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"You have just had lunch," Cayce said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"You are now standing on a street," Cayce said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"You are calling me," Cayce said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"You are calling me," Cayce said, "to recruit me."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"You are recruiting me," Cayce said, "because I am the missing-thing."

"No," Bigend said. "I am recruiting you because you are the substrate. The substrate is what the brand is not. The brand is what is missing from the substrate. The brand is, in this case, what is missing from the missing-thing. The brand is, in this case, what is missing from my missing-thing. The brand is, in this case, the missing-thing in the missing-thing."

Cayce paused.

"The missing-thing in the missing-thing," Cayce said.

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The missing-thing in the missing-thing," Cayce said, "is the brand."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"The brand," Cayce said, "is what you are being hired to identify."

"No," Bigend said. "The brand is what I am not being hired to identify. The brand is what the client is not being hired to identify. The brand is what the substrate is not being hired to identify. The brand is what the missing-thing is not being hired to identify. The brand is what I am not being hired to identify because the brand is, by definition, what cannot be hired. The brand is what can only be identified by the missing-thing. The missing-thing is what you are. You are the missing-thing. You are the substrate-reader. You are the brand-immune. You are the kind of person who can identify what is missing from the substrate. You are, in short, what I am not."

Cayce paused.

"What you are not," Cayce said.

"What I am not," Bigend said.

"What you are not," Cayce said, "is the brand."

"Yes," Bigend said.

"What you are not," Cayce said, "is the missing-thing."

"No," Bigend said. "What I am not is the substrate-reader. What I am not is the brand-immune. What I am not is the kind of person who can identify what is missing from the substrate. What I am not is, in short, what you are."

Cayce paused.

"What you are," Bigend said.
