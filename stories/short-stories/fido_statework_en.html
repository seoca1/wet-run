Fido Statework

The record is never neutral.

Setup

The Sprawl made weather out of money, and the matrix made weather out of fear. Finn sent me after Fido’s statework, with one instruction: bring back a buried municipal contract. The job arrived as a clean line on a dirty screen, an audit or an intrusion depending on who was paying. The mission record called it audit. That was the respectable name. Underneath it sat the real bargain, the one nobody typed into a contract. Someone had built a life around this file, and someone else had decided the life was cheaper than the file. I had seen that arithmetic before. It always began with a small authorization and ended with a person explaining why the missing were part of the plan. The fixer did not mention the names attached to the record. He never did. Names made payment complicated, and complication was the first thing the Sprawl charged interest on.

Rising

I entered through a service node dressed as a maintenance corridor. The map showed offices, depots, clinics, and a narrow channel into the deep system. The map lied in the usual corporate way. It left out the people who kept the lights on. It left out the operators who rented their hands by the hour. It left out the smell of hot insulation and old coffee that followed a signal from one end of the Sprawl to the other. Fido’s statework waited behind a sequence of ordinary permissions. Each permission carried a small piece of ICE, enough to make the system feel less like software and more like a habit. The deeper I went, the more the target looked like a story being forced to wear a spreadsheet. A service worker had left a note in the margins, a warning disguised as a scheduling error. I copied it before the system noticed the shape of the truth. The note was badly spelled and perfectly clear. It said the numbers were not wrong. They were doing exactly what they had been designed to do.

Climax

The defense woke when I touched the final record. A trace that learned to watch back moved across the node without announcing itself. It did not attack the deck first. It attacked the explanation. Every label shifted. Every name acquired a second meaning. The audit became a confession, the confession became an advertisement, and the advertisement became a threat. I held the line while the system tried to make me agree with it. In the corner of the construct, a human voice kept repeating the same question: who benefits when the record is complete? I answered by copying the raw trace before the story could overwrite it. The defense offered me a clean exit if I signed the false version. It offered money, silence, and a future without questions. I chose the damaged file, because damaged files still remember where they were cut. For a moment the system showed me the people behind the entries, not as portraits but as pressure points, each one a place where the account could break.

Resolution

The escape route ran through a sector the fixer had marked as dead. Dead sectors are never dead. They are where the living hide from accounting. I pushed the extracted file through a narrow channel and watched the defense follow. The ICE had learned my route, but it had not learned why I was carrying the data. That gap was enough. I split the package into harmless fragments, sent two toward the public grid, and kept the useful part close to the wetware. The defense chased the decoys. I jack out with a buried municipal contract intact, though the system left a small burn where its explanation had tried to settle. Outside the node, the people named in the record kept working. Their ordinary motion became the only proof that the data was not an abstraction. I carried their unfinished hours with me, a weight no payment schedule had room to list.

Epilogue

Back in the room, the city continued its work. Finn counted the payment before asking whether anyone had been hurt. I gave the answer that kept the next job possible. The file sat between us like a sealed room. It contained no miracle, only a pattern: power calling itself service, memory calling itself product, and ordinary people made responsible for the damage. Outside, the night traffic moved through the Sprawl, across the bridge, or under the blue glow of a shop sign. Somewhere, the target was already being renamed. The record would survive. So would the question. I kept one copy under my own name, which was the closest thing to a promise the job allowed. By morning the client would call the mission successful. The city would call it maintenance. I called it evidence, and waited for the next screen to light.
