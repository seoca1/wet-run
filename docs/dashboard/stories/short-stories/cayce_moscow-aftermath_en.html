Cayce: Moscow Aftermath

736 wordsBlue Ant Trilogy

Hook

"Moscow was where Cayce had finally stopped running from the footage. Moscow was also where she had started running from herself."

Cayce came back from Moscow with a Russian-cold that wouldn't leave her bones and a knowledge she couldn't quite name. She'd been in the city for nine days. She'd met Voytek's people, or Voytek's-people's-people. She'd found the footage's apparent-source, or one of its apparent-sources. She'd come back with more questions than she'd gone with, which was, she realized, the only thing that ever happened when you went looking for footage-origin.

She was in her apartment in Camden Town. The radiator was on but it was the kind of on that meant the landlord had remembered it existed rather than the kind of on that meant the apartment was actually warm. She was wearing two sweaters and drinking tea that had gone cold in the mug.

Rising Action

The footage was still playing on her laptop. She'd been watching it on loop since she'd gotten back, two days ago. She'd been looking for something — a frame, a face, a shape that she recognized. She'd been trying to see Voytek in the footage, even though she knew she wouldn't.

She wasn't going to find him. She knew that now. The footage was not a message. It was not a code. It was not anything that anyone had made for her to find. It was just footage. It was just a piece of the world that someone had pointed a camera at and posted to the internet, and that piece had become the thing that the F:F:F forum spent its time trying to understand.

Cayce had spent years trying to understand it. She'd lost her father to that search. She'd nearly lost herself. She'd been to Moscow, and to Tokyo, and to places that didn't have names on the maps.

The footage kept playing. The girl walked across the plaza. The light changed. The crowd parted.

Midpoint Reversal

Cayce closed the laptop.

She looked at the ceiling. The ceiling was the kind of ceiling that the Camden-Town-landlords put in when they wanted to make a basement look like a flat. It was probably from the 1970s. It had water-stains. It had patterns in the water-stains that were, Cayce thought, more interesting than the patterns in the footage.

She lay down on the sofa. The cushions smelled like the previous-tenant, a smell she hadn't been able to identify for the two years she'd lived here. The cushions smelled like another-life, another-Cayce, a Cayce who hadn't lost anything yet.

Bigend would call her tomorrow. He would ask about the footage. He would ask if she'd found Voytek. He would ask if she wanted to keep working. He would offer her a new-job, a new-investigation, a new-chance to use her allergic-wardrobe-substrate-reading on whatever-emerged.

Climax

Cayce would say no. She was done. She had been done for a while. The footage was not a thing she could solve. It was not a mystery with an answer. It was a substrate-emergence, and she was not a substrate-engineer. She was just a person who could see patterns, and patterns were not answers.

She closed her eyes. She thought about Moscow, about the cold, about the faces of the people she'd met. She thought about her father. She thought about the footage's-girl, walking across the plaza.

She thought: maybe the footage was not a thing. Maybe it was just a moment. Maybe the world had moments, and some of those moments got captured, and some of those captured-moments got watched, and the watching didn't have to mean anything. The watching could just be watching. The footage could just be footage. The girl in the footage could just be a girl.

That was the relief. That was the substrate-shift. That was Cayce's Moscow-aftermath: realizing that some things were not meant to be solved.

Resolution

She lay there for a long time, on the sofa, in the apartment, in the cold. Then she fell asleep.

When she woke up, Bigend had called. The message-light on her phone was blinking. She looked at it for a minute. Then she turned the phone off and went back to sleep.

Tomorrow was another day. Tomorrow was always another day.

"She had spent her life looking for patterns. Now she was learning to let the patterns look for themselves."

The radiator clanked. The ceiling stained. The cushions smelled. Cayce slept.
