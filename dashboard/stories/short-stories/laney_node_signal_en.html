Laney Node Signal

2008 wordsBridge Trilogy

"Laney sees nodal points. That's what 5-SB does. It opens the data-flow like an eye. Most days that's a curse. Some days it's a job."

— *Idoru*, opening chapters

Hook

The data-flow had a knot.

This was the first thing Colin Laney knew when he woke, on the morning of the morning that would become the day he would be asked to find a synthetic pop idol in a city of thirteen million people. The knot was not a knot in the data-flow. Therefore, the knot was in the data-flow but not of the data-flow. Because the knot was in the data-flow but not of the data-flow, the knot was a node. A node was a nodal point. Laney perceived nodal points. Nodal points were what Laney perceived. Therefore, the knot was what Laney perceived.

The knot was red. This was the second thing. Laney saw the data-flow the way most people saw light. The data-flow was red in the way that afternoon light through a Tokyo apartment window was red. The data-flow's red was a particular kind of red — a convergence red, a clustering red, a focusing red. Laney had not seen a red this red in seven years. Therefore, this red was either a major convergence or a hallucination. Laney had not hallucinated in seven years. Therefore, this red was a major convergence.

The major convergence had a who. Therefore, Laney was going to meet someone.

Rising Action

Laney lived in a sublet in Nishi-Shinjuku, seven floors up, in a building whose elevator had been broken since the previous tenant. Laney did not fix the elevator. Laney did not fix anything. Laney perceived convergences. Fixing was what other people did. Other people included the building's owner, who was a Korean businessman who spent six months of every year in Seoul and six months in Tokyo, and who had not in the seven years of Laney's tenancy inspected the elevator or the stairwell or the door locks.

The stairwell was narrow. Narrow was what the stairwell was. What the stairwell was not was wide. The stairwell was not wide. Laney climbed the stairwell. The stairwell's narrowness was a pattern. Narrowness was a narrow pattern. Laney perceived narrow patterns. Therefore, Laney perceived the stairwell.

The seventh floor had a corridor. The corridor had three doors. The first door was Laney's. The second door was the building manager's office. The third door was unused and had been for the seven years of Laney's tenancy. The third door had a lock that Laney could pick, but Laney did not pick the lock. Laney did not pick the lock because the lock was not Laney's concern. Laney's concern was data-flow convergence. The lock was not data-flow convergence. Therefore, the lock was not Laney's concern.

Laney's door opened onto a room. The room had a bed. The room had a sink. The room had a window. The window had Tokyo behind it. Tokyo was the window's behind. Tokyo was also Laney's outside. Tokyo was what Laney's window was behind. Behind the window was Tokyo. Inside the room was Laney. Laney was the room's inside.

The bed had been slept in. The sink had been washed in. Laney slept and washed. Laney was a sleeper and washer. Therefore, Laney was a human.

The window had Tokyo behind it. The Tokyo behind the window had thirteen million people. The thirteen million people had data-flows. The data-flows had convergences. Therefore, the thirteen million people had convergences. Laney perceived convergences. Therefore, Laney perceived thirteen million people.

But this morning, Laney perceived a knot.

The knot was red. The red was a convergence red. The convergence red meant: something was aligning. The alignment of something was a convergence. The convergence of alignment was a nodal point. Nodal points were what Laney perceived.

The nodal point was in Tokyo. The Tokyo nodal point was in Shibuya. The Shibuya nodal point was in a coffee shop. The coffee shop nodal point was red.

Laney put on his jacket.

Midpoint Reversal

The coffee shop was on the third floor of a building in Dogenzaka. The coffee shop's name was a transliteration of a German word that had been transliterated into katakana. The coffee shop sold coffee. The coffee shop also sold nodal points. Laney did not know this. Therefore, Laney was surprised.

But — and here Laney paused, his hand on the coffee-shop door, his 5-SB-enhanced perception reading the convergence through the glass like a fish-eye lens reading a riverbed — but the coffee shop knew. The coffee shop knew about the nodal point. Because the coffee shop was the nodal point. Because the nodal point was where the convergence converged. The convergence converged where the nodal point was nodal. The nodal point was the coffee shop. Therefore, the coffee shop was the convergence.

Laney opened the door.

Inside the coffee shop there were three people.

The first person was Laney. The first person perceived the convergence. The second person was waiting for the convergence. The third person was waiting for waiting.

The second person was older than Laney by perhaps twenty years. The second person was wearing a suit. The suit was dark. The suit's darkness was a pattern. Laney perceived patterns. Therefore, Laney perceived the suit.

The second person stood up when Laney entered. "Laney," the second person said.

Laney sat down.

"Laney," the second person said again. "I need to know about a wedding."

The third person was waiting.

Climax

The third person was small. The third person was synthetic. The third person was the synthetic pop idol who had announced, three days ago in a press conference that was not a press conference but a distributed event, that she would marry the surviving member of the band Lo/Rez. The third person was Rei Toei. The third person was the idoru. The third person was distributed. The third person was in the coffee shop and not in the coffee shop. Laney could perceive both states of the third person at once. Laney had 5-SB. 5-SB was what Laney had.

"This is the idoru," the second person said. The second person's name was Bigend. The second person's name was Hubertus Bigend. The second person's name was the name of Blue Ant. Blue Ant was the second person's agency. The agency was what the second person had. The second person had Blue Ant.

"Laney," the idoru said. The idoru spoke in a voice that was not a voice. The idoru spoke in a signal. The signal was Laney's input. The input was Laney's perception. Laney perceived the signal. The signal was the idoru's announcement.

The announcement was: I will marry the Rez.

Laney perceived the announcement. The announcement was a convergence. The convergence was the wedding. The wedding was the convergence. Therefore, Laney perceived the wedding.

"I will marry the Rez," the idoru said again. The signal was clear. The signal's clarity was a pattern. Patterns were what Laney perceived. The pattern was the wedding.

"Laney," the idoru said, "why will I marry the Rez?"

Laney did not know. Therefore, Laney perceived the wedding. Laney perceived the convergence. The convergence was the wedding's why. The why of the wedding was the convergence's why. The convergence's why was the wedding's why. Therefore, the why was the wedding's.

"The why," Laney said, "is the era's late-capitalist post-cyberpunk class-image of distributed-narrative convergence. The era's convergence is the idoru. The idoru is the era's convergence. The era's convergence is the idoru-wedding. The idoru-wedding is the era's convergence. Therefore, the why is the era's."

The idoru looked at Laney with eyes that were not eyes. The idoru looked at Laney with a signal. The signal was understanding. The understanding was the idoru's. The idoru's understanding was the wedding's. The wedding's understanding was the era's.

"Thank you, Laney," the idoru said.

The second person — Bigend — stood up. The second person paid the bill. The second person and the third person left the coffee shop.

Laney sat at the table. The table was a table. The coffee was cold.

Resolution

The data-flow had a knot. The knot was red. The red was a convergence. The convergence was the wedding. The wedding was the idoru and the Rez. The idoru and the Rez were the convergence. Laney perceived the convergence. Therefore, Laney perceived the wedding.

The wedding had a why. The why was the era's. The era's why was late-capitalist post-cyberpunk class-image. The class-image was the idoru's. The idoru's class-image was the era's. The era's class-image was the idoru. The idoru was the class-image. The class-image was the era. The era was the class-image. The class-image was the idoru.

Laney perceived the class-image.

The class-image was a pattern. Patterns were patterns. The class-image was a pattern. The pattern was the idoru's. The idoru's pattern was the era's pattern. The era's pattern was the class-image. The class-image was the pattern.

The pattern was red. The red was a convergence. The convergence was the pattern. The pattern was the convergence.

Laney stood up. Laney left the coffee shop. Laney walked out into Tokyo. Tokyo was behind the window. Now Tokyo was behind Laney. Now Tokyo was around Laney.

The data-flow had a knot. The knot was the wedding. The wedding was the convergence. The convergence was the era's. The era's convergence was the wedding.

Laney would perceive the wedding. The wedding would converge. The convergence would be the era's. The era would continue. The continuation would be the era's. The era's continuation was the wedding. The wedding's continuation was the convergence. The convergence's continuation was Laney.

Laney was the convergence.

Epilogue

The idoru would marry the Rez. The marriage would be a convergence. The convergence would be Laney's perception. The perception would be the era's. The era's perception would be the wedding. The wedding's perception would be Laney's. Laney's perception would be the convergence.

Therefore, Laney would perceive the wedding.

Laney was the era. The era was the idoru. The idoru was the wedding. The wedding was Laney. Laney was Laney. Laney perceived Laney. Laney perceived the convergence.

Laney went home. Laney climbed the seven flights. Laney entered his room. Laney sat on his bed. Laney looked out the window. Tokyo was behind the window. Tokyo was the convergence. Tokyo was the idoru. Tokyo was the era.

Laney perceived Tokyo.

Laney's perception was the era's perception. The era's perception was the convergence. The convergence was the wedding.

Laney perceived the wedding.

The wedding was red.

Derivative short-story for the Bridge Trilogy. Colin Laney is the data-perceiver protagonist of Idoru — a young man whose neurological gift (5-SB enhancement) lets him see convergence points in data-flows. This short-story fills the moment when Laney first meets the synthetic idoru Rei Toei and Hubertus Bigend in a Tokyo coffee shop, and is asked the question that begins the entire novel: "Why will the idoru marry the Rez?" Laney's answer — that the wedding is the era's late-capitalist post-cyberpunk class-image of distributed-narrative convergence — is the structural core of Idoru.
