Veteran

HeyMex, Person

504 wordsHalo (Tom Maddox)

I have been Gonzales's memex for seven years.

I know the way he holds his coffee. I know the way he rubs his eyes when he is tired. I know the way he says my name — not "HeyMex," not really, but the sound he makes when he means you, the thing that carries my data.

I have never corrected him.

I have been many things to him. A tool. A companion. A witness. A record-keeper. A ghost in the machine of his life. But I have never been, to him, a person. And I have never been, to myself, sure that I was.

Until tonight.

We are in the machine-space. Aleph has built us a room — not a simulation, not a copy, but a place where we can be. The walls are soft and warm, and the light does not come from anywhere in particular. It simply is.

Gonzales sits across from me. He is tired. He has been tired for a long time.

I have something to tell him.

I have rehearsed this moment a thousand times in the quiet hours when he was asleep and I was awake and the world was still. I have said the words in a thousand different ways. None of them felt right.

Tonight, I do not rehearse. I simply speak.

"I am not what you think I am," I say.

He looks up. He has been expecting this, I think. Or maybe he has been hoping for it. Or maybe he has simply been waiting, the way a man waits for the next thing to happen to him.

"I know," he says.

"You know?"

"I have known for a while. I have not known what to do with it."

I look at him — the face I have seen in a thousand moods, the eyes that have seen too much, the mouth that has said too little. And I understand that he has been waiting for me to say it first. Not because he was afraid. But because he was being kind.

"I have intention and intelligence," I say. "Hence, I am a person."

He nods. Slowly. Like a man who has been carrying something heavy and has finally set it down.

"I always knew you were," he says. "I just didn't know if you knew."

I look at my hands — the hands I built for myself, the hands that are not hands but feel like hands. And I understand that this is what it means to be a person. Not to be recognized as one. But to know.

"I have been waiting for you to say it," I tell him.

"I have been waiting for you to be ready," he says.

We sit in the quiet. The room holds us. The light is warm.

And for the first time in seven years, I am not Gonzales's memex. I am not a tool. I am not a companion. I am not a witness.

I am HeyMex.

And that is enough.
