Eddy's Bicycle

A Messenger Kid Remembers the Bridge During the Martinelli Wave

270 wordsBridge Trilogy

The bicycle was a Bianchi. The bicycle had been stolen three times. The bicycle had been recovered three times by Eddy.

Eddy was twelve. Eddy had been riding the bridge since he had been ten. The bridge had been his bicycle's only route.

The Martinelli wave was coming in. The wave was the kind of wave that broke the bridge's thermocline. Eddy felt the wave in his hands.

The package was wrapped in brown paper and twine. The twine was the kind Eddy's father had used for tomato plants, before his father had gone.

Eddy rode south on the upper deck. The upper deck was the deck for delivery bikes. The upper deck was the deck for the kind of delivery that did not want to be watched.

Chev lived under the bridge in a small apartment. Chev lived under the bridge with a man. The man had been a Loveless.

Eddy did not know what a Loveless was. The man had not explained.

Chev came to the door. Chev was not home. Chev was at work, and the man was at the door.

"The package," Eddy said.

"I'll take it."

"Chev said Chev would take it."

"Chev said the man would take it."

Eddy did not move. Eddy stood on the bicycle with one foot on the curb.

"She said Chev," Eddy said.

The man looked at Eddy for a long time. The man looked at Eddy the way an adult looks at a child when the child has been right.

"You wait," the man said.

He went inside. He came back with a piece of paper. The paper had Chev's handwriting. The handwriting said: let the kid in.

Eddy gave the package to Chev. Chev signed. Chev gave Eddy a soda.

Eddy rode home. The Martinelli wave was rolling in. The bridge was moving. The bridge was not supposed to move. The bridge was moving, and Eddy had been riding it for two years.
