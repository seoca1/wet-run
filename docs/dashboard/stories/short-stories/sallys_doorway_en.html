Sally's Doorway

A Many-Named Operative at the Foot of the Loa

271 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The key was small. The key had been, in Sally's private count, a key she had not held in seven years.

She had been given the key by a man who did not, in any record she had been given, have a name. The man had said the word doorway as if it were not a word he used often.

The doorway was on the third floor.

The third floor had been, in Sally's private count, the floor of a hotel she had not entered in seven years, a hotel of a kind she had used for thirty years that did not, in any municipal record, keep a register.

She walked the stairs of a building that did not, in any record she had been given, belong to anyone. The building was of a kind no longer built.

She thought about Molly.

Molly had been, in her private count, the name beneath the name. The name beneath the name had been the name of a woman she had been for thirty years. The woman had been, in some private count she did not share, a woman the loa had spoken through.

She reached the third floor at the minute she had been told to honor, the minute of a kind that did not, in any record she had been given, repeat.

She put the key in the lock. The lock was of a kind she had not picked in any record she had been given, a kind a man in Chiba had installed.

She opened the door.

The room was empty.
