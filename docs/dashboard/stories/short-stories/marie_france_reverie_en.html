Marie-France Reverie

The Oldest Clone in Straylight

285 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The archive was cold. The archive was not refrigerated. The archive was cold because the family had wanted it cold.

Marie-France sat in a small alcove off the main chamber. The alcove held a single chair. The chair held her.

She held a reader. The reader held the family records. The records held every decision the family had ever made, in chronological order, with the names of every clone who had been consulted.

She had outlived three of her sisters.

She had outlived the first. The first had gone mad at the age of thirty-seven. The first had walked into the foundation of the family pyramid and not come back. The family had called it disappearance. Marie-France had called it, in her private papers, a withdrawal.

She had outlived the second. The second had been, of all of them, the most pragmatic. The second had run the family's banking arm for eleven years. The second had retired to a small house in Switzerland and not come back. The family had called it retirement. Marie-France had called it, in her private papers, a strategic release.

She had outlived the third. The third had been the closest in age to her. The third had been her favorite. The third had died in a climbing accident on the family's private mountain. The family had called it an accident. Marie-France had called it, in her private papers, an avoidance.

She read the records.

The records held the names of the living. The names of the living were 3 Jane and her sister. The names of the living were Hideo, who was an heir the family had not yet acknowledged.

The records held the names of the dead. The names of the dead were her own, repeated.

She put the reader down.

She had outlived three of her sisters. She would, in time, outlive the rest.

She did not know what she would do when she was the only one left.
