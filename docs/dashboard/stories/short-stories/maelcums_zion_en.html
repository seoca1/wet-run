Maelcum's Zion

A Loa Rasta on the Marcus Garvey

286 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The Garvey was small. The Garvey was his.

Maelcum sat in the cockpit with his feet on the dashboard. The dashboard was a long thin strip of green-lit panels. The panels showed the status of the bio. The panels showed the status of the electrical. The panels showed the status of the loa, which the panels could not show, but which the Garvey crew knew by other signs.

Maelcum listened to the loa.

The loa spoke in Jamaican. The loa spoke through the creak of the airlock. The loa spoke through the rhythm of the recycling pumps. The loa spoke in the small sounds of a ship that had been kept alive for forty years by a crew that did not believe in throwing things away.

"I and I," Maelcum said. "We are here."

A voice in the back. Aerol, who was not yet old, who was going to fly with Case, who was afraid and not saying so.

"Brother Maelcum."

"Brother."

"Will the cowboys come tonight?"

"They come when they come. They come when the loa send them."

Aerol was quiet for a while.

"Brother Maelcum."

"Brother."

"What if they don't come?"

"Then we wait."

Aerol was quiet again.

Maelcum listened to the loa. The loa were patient. The loa had been patient for a long time. The loa had been patient through the Fall, through the Exodus, through the years in low orbit when the only thing the cluster had been owed was silence.

"We wait," Maelcum said. "We wait. We are not yet owed anything but the wait."

The panels showed the bio. The panels showed the electrical. The loa, who were not on the panels, kept their own counsel.

Aerol leaned against the bulkhead.

The Garvey was small. The Garvey was his. The Garvey was, for tonight, the whole of Zion that he could see.
