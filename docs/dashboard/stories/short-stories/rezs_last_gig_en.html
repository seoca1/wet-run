Rez's Last Gig

A Singer Without a Band Walks Off the Bridge

294 wordsBridge Trilogy

The rehearsal room was above the bridge. The room had been rented by a man who had not said his name.

Rez set up his guitar. The guitar had been, in his private count, the guitar of a kind he had not played since Lo died.

He had been told, by Bigend, that Lo was finished. He had been told this at a meeting in a hotel.

Rez had asked, in the meeting, if he would sing again. Bigend had said nothing.

Rez played three chords. The chords had been, in his private count, the chords of a kind that did not ask permission.

He played them again.

The room had been empty. The empty had been the empty of a kind that did not require a witness.

He played the bridge. He put the guitar down.

He walked to the window. The window had been, in his private count, the window of a kind that opened onto the Bay.

Below, the bridge went on. The bridge had been, in his private count, the bridge of a kind that did not stop carrying weight.

Rez put the guitar back in its case. He had decided, in his private count, to sing again tomorrow.

He left the room. He had said, in his private count, the last word Bigend had not said for him.

He walked down the bridge. The walking had been, in his private count, the walking of a kind that did not require a hotel.

He had thought about Lo. The thinking had been the thinking of a kind that did not require a witness.

He had walked home. The home had been, in his private count, the home of a kind that did not mind the music.
