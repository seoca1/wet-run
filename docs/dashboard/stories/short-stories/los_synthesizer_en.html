Lo's Synthesizer

An Absent Sound Architect Is Found in the Module's Wiring

258 wordsBridge Trilogy

The technician opened the synthesizer on a table. The table had been, in his private count, the table of a kind no longer made.

The synthesizer had been Lo's synthesizer. The synthesizer had been Lo's for nine years. The nine years had been the years Lo had been missing.

The case was a flight case built by a man in Kanda who had died in 2007.

The technician opened the case. Inside, the wiring was hand-soldered. The solder had been the solder of a kind no longer sold in Tokyo.

The technician took photographs. The photographs were the first photographs of the inside of the synthesizer. The first photographs had not, in any record he had been given, been authorized.

The Rez arrived. The Rez had been the Rez without Lo. The Rez had been, in his private count, the only person authorized to see what the technician was photographing.

"Don't," the Rez said.

"I'm not stopping," the technician said.

"Don't post them."

"I will not post them."

The technician took three more photographs of a wire from the oscillator to the filter. The wire had been the wire of a kind made by Lo.

The Rez sat down. The Rez had been sitting down since 2008.

"It still works," the technician said.

"It still works," the Rez said.

The technician closed the case. The Rez carried the case to the door.

"Don't take more photographs," the Rez said.

"I will not take more photographs."

The Rez walked into the street. The synthesizer still worked.
