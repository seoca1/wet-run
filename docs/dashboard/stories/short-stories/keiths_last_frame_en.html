Keith's Last Frame

A Freelance Journalist Saves the Footage for the Morning

271 wordsBridge Trilogy

The footage had been on the laptop for nine hours. Keith had been awake for twenty.

He sat at the desk the hotel had given him. The desk faced the window.

The footage was the interview. The interview had been with Colin Laney. Laney had been a man who did not want to be interviewed.

Laney had wanted to talk about Tokyo. Tokyo had been a city. Keith had pressed record.

The interview had lasted forty minutes. The minutes had been the minutes of a man who could see things no one else could see.

Laney had described the Idoru. The Idoru had been the Idoru of a kind that was not, in any record he had been given, a person.

Keith had asked three questions. The questions had been the questions of a journalist who had not yet been paid.

Laney had answered. The answer had been the answer of a man who had been paid in something other than money.

The interview had ended. Laney had left. The leaving had been the leaving of a man who had said what he had come to say.

Keith watched the last frame. The frame had been the frame of a kind that did not show Laney's face. The face had been the face of a man who had given Keith the only frame Keith would be allowed to keep.

He drank the coffee. The coffee had been the coffee of a kind that was not, in any record he had been given, good.

He saved the file. The file went to the editor at six.

He slept.
