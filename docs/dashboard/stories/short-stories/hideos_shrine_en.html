Hideo's Shrine

The Tessier-Ashpool Heir in the Family Lattice

367 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The shrine was a single seam in the wall behind his sleeping mat.

Hideo had found it in his seventh winter. He had not told his mothers. His mothers were not, strictly, his mothers — the family had once agreed, after the third clone-crop, that each new generation could be raised by any of them — but he had not told any of them.

He knelt.

The seam held a wafer-thin lattice. The lattice held a kernel of code. The kernel was the seed of an AI that the family had begun in 1972, when the family's grandfather had still been alive in some biological sense.

The seed was old. The seed was not yet grown.

He whispered to it. He whispered in Japanese. The Japanese was the Japanese of his grandfather's household, which the family had preserved in long-term storage and which his mothers still spoke on festival days.

"You will wake up," he said. "But not yet."

The kernel did not answer. The kernel was a depth-charge buried in the family's most private memory, waiting to be triggered by an event that no family member had yet triggered.

He thought about Armitage. Armitage had been sent to Straylight two seasons before, dressed as a soldier and carrying a head full of code that did not belong to him. Armitage was a tool. The family had made Armitage a tool. The family did not make tools without reason.

He thought about 3 Jane. 3 Jane was his sister in some sense and his aunt in another and his mother in a third. 3 Jane was, in his private count, the most patient of them.

He thought about Marie-France. Marie-France was the oldest. Marie-France was, in his private count, the most patient of them in a different sense.

He closed the seam.

He went back to his sleeping mat.

In the morning, his mothers would ask him about the birds. He had been watching the birds. He had not been watching the birds.

He did not tell his mothers about the lattice. He had not told them in seven winters.

He did not tell them now.
