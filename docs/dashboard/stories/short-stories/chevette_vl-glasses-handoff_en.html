Chevette: VL Glasses Handoff

819 wordsBridge Trilogy

Hook

"She'd touched them once, in the kiosk, before WarBaby had noticed her. The gesture-language was like a song whose lyrics she couldn't quite make out, but whose beat she could feel in her wrists."

The VL glasses were still in her jacket pocket when WarBaby found her on the bridge at dusk. He didn't grab for them. He didn't raise his voice. He just stood there in his black-on-black, his face half-lit by the dying-sun coming through the suspension-cables, and waited.

"I took them," Chevette said. "I know I shouldn't have."

WarBaby nodded once, slow. "Why?"

She didn't know how to answer. She didn't understand her own reasons. She'd been drawn to the glasses, in the kiosk, the way she was drawn to certain people on the bridge — not because they were beautiful or useful or good, but because they were something, they had a charge, they were alive in a way the rest of the city wasn't.

Rising Action

"The gesture-language," she said finally. "It was like... I don't know. Like someone had figured out a way to talk without using words."

WarBaby's expression didn't change. He was good at not changing his expression. Skinner had told her once: WarBaby's face was a controlled-environment; nothing got out that he hadn't put there.

"And you wanted to talk," WarBaby said.

Chevette thought about it. The answer was yes, and it was more than yes. She had grown up on the bridge, surrounded by people who talked with their hands, who used the bridge's own gesture-vocabulary — wave-across, point-down, palm-up, all those unspoken-bridge-dialects. The VL glasses were an extension of that, she realized. They were a formalization of what the bridge had always done.

"I wanted to wear them," she said. "Just for a minute. Just to feel what it was like."

WarBaby stepped closer. She could smell his cologne — something expensive, something that didn't belong on the bridge.

Midpoint Reversal

"Then wear them," he said.

He held them out. They were warm from his hand. Chevette took them with a strange-caution, the way you'd take a loaded-weapon you didn't fully understand.

She put them on.

The bridge changed. Not visually — the optics were subtle, a kind of ghost-overlay on top of the real-bridge, the real-fog, the real-Lost-Norters moving through their evening-routines. But Chevette could feel the gesture-language now, as if it had always been there, just below the threshold of perception. The guy waving to his girlfriend at the next-bay had a glyph-flowing off his hand. The kid climbing the suspension-cable had a tag hovering at his waist. The Armenian-vendor had a small-menu-thing orbiting his head, in a language Chevette didn't read but understood the meaning of.

She could feel the bridge being alive in a way she hadn't known it could be alive.

She took the glasses off.

Climax

"Thank you," she said.

WarBaby took them back. "You get why they're worth killing for, now."

"Yeah."

"And you get why they'll get us all killed if the wrong people take them."

"Yeah."

He nodded. "Then we have a deal. You keep your mouth shut. I give you a job."

Hook

Resolution

"What job?"

"Carrying things. People forget to look at girls carrying bags. They look at me, they look at other-carryers. They don't look at you."

Chevette looked at him. WarBaby's face was still that controlled-environment, that locked-room. But she thought she could see something behind it — something that might have been fear, or might have been hope.

Rising Action

"Okay," she said.

"Okay," he said.

He walked away. Chevette watched him go, his black-on-black disappearing into the dusk and the fog and the bridge's slow-evening-shift. Then she went to find Skinner, because Skinner needed to know, and Skinner always knew what to do with information like this.

But for a few minutes, on the bridge, in the fading-light, she just stood there with her hands in her jacket pockets, where the VL glasses had been, and felt the ghost of their gesture-language still humming in her wrists.

"The bridge had taught her many things. Most of them she couldn't name. But this — that the city below wanted to kill for a language of hands — this, she could name."
