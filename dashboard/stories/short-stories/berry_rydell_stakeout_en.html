Berry Rydell, Bounty Stakeout

1247 wordsBridge Trilogy

"Inconvenience stalked him, but the wages kept his lights on and his bike in one piece."

— Virtual Light, Berry Rydell's interior monologue

도입 (Setup)

Berry Rydell had been watching the same corner of Telegraph Avenue for six hours when the rain started. The shelter over his head was a defunct awning whose screen still flickered with the ghost of a SoftCell ad — a woman, mid-laugh, holding a phone he no longer recognized as a phone. The street had changed in the time he had watched it. A kiosk that had advertised coffee that morning had been replaced by one that advertised a tea he had never heard of. The tea's Japanese characters had been drawn by a hand that, Berry suspected, had never touched a teacup.

His target was a woman named Halliday, who had skipped bail on a fraud charge three weeks earlier. Halliday was a mid-level data-broker from a courier company whose name Berry could not pronounce. The company's logo, when Berry had last seen it, had been a small black cube. Berry had been hired by someone who had hired someone. Berry was three contractuals deep into a chain that led back to a name on a name on a name. The name at the bottom, Berry had been told by someone who seemed to know, was Lowbeer.

Lowbeer had not asked Berry to find Halliday. Lowbeer had, according to the version Berry had received, asked someone who had asked someone. Berry had been given a photograph. Berry had been given an address. Berry had been given the name of a bar where Halliday liked to drink.

Berry had not been given the reason Lowbeer wanted Halliday found.

전개 (Rising Action)

The bar was a place called The Settlement, three blocks south of the bridge. Berry had walked past it twice that afternoon and once that evening. The Settlement's front window was a screen that played, at any given moment, one of seven looping scenes of nineteenth-century Oakland. The scenes were stylized and slow. Berry had watched the same carriage travel the same street, in the same direction, for forty minutes. The carriage had been pulled by the same horse. The horse had been the same horse. Berry had begun to feel that he had watched this horse for his entire life.

At eleven, Halliday emerged. She was smaller than Berry had expected. Her hair was short. Her jacket was the kind Berry associated with people who worked in data — pockets too many, fabric too stiff. Halliday walked north on Telegraph, toward the bridge. Berry followed at the distance he had been taught to maintain — close enough to see, far enough not to be seen. The distance, in the post-quake Oakland, was wider than it had been in pre-quake Oakland. People walked faster. People walked less.

The bridge loomed above them. The bridge was what Oakland had become. Berry had been told that the bridge's underside housed a community of people who had nowhere else to go. Berry had been told that the community had a courier named Chevette who ran data-keys across the bridge at night. Berry had not been told whether Chevette was a real person or a story the bridge told about itself.

Berry watched Halliday enter the bridge's lower-deck service corridor. Berry counted to thirty. Berry followed.

The service corridor smelled like wet concrete and human presence. The corridor was lit by fixtures whose covers had been replaced, several times, by materials whose origin Berry could not guess. The light was the color of TV static. Berry walked. Berry watched. Halliday walked ahead of him, her shoulders' rhythm slightly irregular — the rhythm of someone who knows she is being followed, or who fears she might be.

절정 (Climax)

At the corridor's fourth bend, Halliday stopped. Halliday turned.

"You can come out," Halliday said. "I know you've been on me since the bar."

Berry stepped from the alcove where the alcove had hidden him. Berry's hand was near his pocket, where the pocket held the device that would let him call for the police. Berry did not reach for the device. Berry had been taught that reaching was the first thing a target noticed.

"You don't look like a cop," Halliday said.

"I'm not a cop," Berry said.

"You don't look like a finder, either."

"I'm a finder," Berry said.

Halliday looked at Berry for a long moment. The corridor's wet-metal light moved slightly. Berry realized he had been told to find a person, and the person was here, and the person was not afraid, and Berry did not know what came next.

"What does Lowbeer want?" Halliday said.

Berry said nothing. Berry did not know. Berry had not been told. The finding was the finding; the why was the why.

"I can guess," Halliday said. "She wants the name."

"The name," Berry said.

"The name I was hiding behind the fraud. The name I was running from."

Berry did not move.

"She already knows," Halliday said. "She has always known. She's having you find me so I can tell you."

Berry's hand relaxed by his pocket. Berry's hand returned to his side.

"Tell me, then," Berry said.

Halliday took a step toward Berry. Halliday's voice, when she spoke, was quiet enough that Berry had to lean in to hear it. The corridor's acoustics swallowed most of what Halliday said. Berry heard only fragments. A name Berry did not recognize. A relationship Lowbeer had with someone Berry had never heard of. A data-key that had been moving, for years, between parties Berry had not known existed.

Berry listened. Berry did not write anything down. Berry's training was clear: the finding is the finding; the listening is the listening; the report is the report.

결말 (Resolution)

At the corridor's far end, Halliday turned and walked back toward Telegraph. Berry watched her go. Berry did not follow. The finding was complete. The why was for someone else.

Berry walked the rest of the corridor alone. Berry emerged onto a different street than the one he had entered. Berry's bike, where he had chained it, was gone. Berry's wallet, in his jacket, was missing the cash that had been in the wallet. Berry's device, in his pocket, was intact. Berry's mind was a different shape than it had been six hours earlier.

Berry called the device's number. A voice Berry did not recognize answered. Berry reported: Halliday found. Halliday cooperative. Halliday returned to her own route. The name obtained. The name spoken only to the device.

Berry waited. The device's voice said: Acknowledged. Your fee has been deposited.

Berry put the device away. Berry looked at the bridge. Berry thought about Chevette, the courier, who Berry had been told was a story the bridge told about itself. Berry thought about Lowbeer, who had hired Berry through three levels of name. Berry thought about the data-key Halliday had described, and the parties that had been moving it, and the years the parties had been moving it.

The bridge held the sky the way the sky held the bridge. Berry walked toward the bridge's south ramp. Berry walked because the walk was what Berry could do now.
