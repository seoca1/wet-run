The Alibi-Fixer: Flynne Meets Wilf Lowbeer

Origin: Flynne meeting Wilf Netherton / Lowbeer in the stub

937 wordsPeripheral

He was sitting at a small round table in a corner of a tea shop that didn't exist in any tea shop I'd ever been in, in a city that didn't exist in any decade I'd ever lived in, drinking something out of a cup that had no handle. The tea shop was on the second floor of a building that had a green awning out front, and the awning was the kind of green that I had only ever seen on the side panel of a library book or on the felt of a card table at the volunteer fire department.

The man at the table was older than I'd expected. I had not known what to expect, but I had expected someone closer to Conner's age. He was closer to my mother's age. He had a face that had been folded and refolded by the same wind for a long time, and his hands were the hands of someone who had done something careful with them for many years, the way a piano player's hands look like piano hands, except his hands looked like alibi hands. I didn't know what alibi hands were supposed to look like. I was about to find out.

"Ms. Fisher," he said.

"Sir."

"You don't have to call me sir. Wilf."

"Wilf."

"Wilf Netherton, formally. Wilf Lowbeer, informally, among people who know me better than you do. You will be among the people who know me better than you do before too long."

"Sit down," he said.

I sat down.

The chair I sat down in was made of a kind of wood that I had never seen before. It was a very dark brown with a fine grain and it was warm to the touch, which was strange because the tea shop was air-conditioned to a temperature that I would have called "slightly cool" in Clanton and that I suspected was "slightly warm" by the standards of wherever this tea shop actually was.

"Tea?" he said.

"I don't know what kind of tea you have."

"We have what you would call sweet tea. We also have what you would call unsweet tea. The difference between them is structural."

"The difference is sugar."

"The difference is structural, Ms. Fisher. The sugar is added in both cases, in different quantities, by different methods, at different stages. The unsweet tea is unsweetened in the way that you mean, but the sweet tea is sweetened by a method that we could not replicate in your timeline without equipment that doesn't exist in your timeline."

"Wilf."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"I am sitting in a tea shop in a city that is not the city I was born in, in a body that is not the body I was born in, drinking tea that has been sweetened by a method I will not be told about, by a man whose last name I do not yet understand."

"That is a fair summary."

"Why did you want to meet me in person."

"I am an alibi-fixer, Ms. Fisher. I arrange the circumstances under which improbable events become credible. I do not arrange events. I arrange the alibis for events."

"The alibis."

"Yes."

"The alibi for what."

"For whatever happens in the body you are currently sitting in. For whatever happens in the bodies of the people who will be operating in this city and in your timeline under the cover of you operating in this body. For the fact that a woman from Clanton is walking around in a London peripheral at four in the morning local time and that this woman is, in some sense, the operator of a thing that will rearrange the probability of several centuries."

"Wilf."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"I am the operator of a thing."

"Yes."

"I am the operator of a thing that will rearrange the probability of several centuries, and you are the man who fixes the alibis, and we are sitting in a tea shop in a city that is not the city I was born in drinking tea that has been sweetened by a method I will not be told about, and you are doing all of this with your face folded in a particular way that I have not yet learned to read."

"That is also a fair summary."

"Wilf."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"What do I call you. Wilf, or Wilf Netherton, or Wilf Lowbeer, or sir, or something else."

"You can call me Wilf."

"Just Wilf."

"Just Wilf."

"Wilf, I am from Clanton, West Virginia. I work at Houdini's Foods. I am sitting in this tea shop because my brother is in trouble and you are the person who can fix the trouble, or arrange the alibi for the trouble being fixed, or whatever it is you actually do. I have been in this peripheral for four sessions and I have learned three things. I have learned that the tea here is sweeter than the tea at home. I have learned that the sky here is sometimes a different color than the sky at home. And I have learned that you are the person who is going to be telling me what the operator-of-a-thing actually does."

"That is also a fair summary, Ms. Fisher."

"Wilf."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"Stop calling me Ms. Fisher."

"Flynne."

"Yes."

"Flynne, the operator-of-a-thing does whatever the operator-of-a-thing is asked to do. Sometimes that is sitting in a chair. Sometimes that is walking. Sometimes that is drinking tea in a tea shop with an alibi-fixer who is older than the operator-of-a-thing."

"Wilf."

"Yes, Flynne."

"I am going to need more tea."
