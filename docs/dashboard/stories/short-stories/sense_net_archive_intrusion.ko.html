Sense/Net Archive Intrusion

Introduction

*"The matrix is an abstract representation of the relationships between data systems."*

— Gibson, Neuromancer, Chapter 2

The Suit had not been asleep. The Suit rarely slept in any meaningful sense — three hours, a glass of warm milk, a closed-circuit news feed running at low volume, the rest of his nervous system tuned to the frequencies of corporate-statework. He was at his desk in the Sprawl Hole when the call came in, working through a manifest of Sense/Net credentials that had been voided that morning by a routine security update he had not been informed of. The call rang twice before he picked it up. He picked it up because the number on the caller ID belonged to a man who did not make social calls.

Development

"Sense/Net archive, three minutes ago," the voice said. No identification, no greeting. The voice was Slick Henry's voice, which is to say: the voice of a man who had been running corporate-statework for thirty years and had stopped using names because names made you traceable. "Three Cowboy-class operators. Not hustler-class. Not the working-class hounds. Three professionals. They've been inside the first-defense perimeter for almost an hour."

The Suit said nothing for a moment. He set down the manifest. He picked up a separate terminal — older, smaller, its case the color of bone — and entered a string of characters that Sense/Net had issued him eleven years ago, and that Sense/Net had then, by virtue of having been issued and forgotten, lost the ability to revoke. The terminal woke. The terminal asked him who he was. He told the terminal.

"I'm in," he said.

He saw the Sense/Net archive resolve around him. The first-defense perimeter, which Slick Henry had described, was not actually a perimeter. It was a series of perimeters nested inside each other like the rings of an onion, each one shaped slightly differently, each one keyed to a different layer of corporate fear. The Suit had walked through these perimeters before. He knew their shape. He knew the seams.

The three Cowboy-class operators, when he found them, were working a corner of the third ring. They were good. Their approach was layered, with one running interference at the second ring, two more actively breaching, all three of them riding what looked like a single compiled intrusion suite. The Suit watched them for ninety seconds before he moved. In those ninety seconds he noted their tools, their protocols, the way their fingers moved across imaginary keyboards, the way their compiled suite was structured around an assumption that the Sense/Net archive would behave like every other corporate archive he had ever known. He was betting they would be wrong about that.

Climax

The Suit moved through the rings. He did not breach them. He did not need to. He walked through them, because the credentials in his bone-colored terminal were of the kind that Sense/Net had issued before it understood what those credentials meant. He moved behind the three operators. He reached the archive's central record.

The operators were looking for something. He could tell what it was, now that he was close. They were looking for a particular fragment of Sense/Net's memory — a fragment that had been flagged, ten years ago, as the kind of thing that Sense/Net did not want to remember, and so had been moved to a part of the archive that, officially, did not exist. The three operators had been told the fragment was there. The Suit did not know by whom. He did know that whoever had told them had known exactly where to look, which meant they were either Sense/Net themselves, or they were someone who had once been Sense/Net and had left with knowledge they were not supposed to leave with.

The Suit did not let them reach the fragment.

He did not confront them. He did not raise an alarm. He did something simpler and more elegant: he moved the fragment. From the part of the archive that did not officially exist, he moved the fragment into the part of the archive that did, where it would be re-indexed, re-catalogued, and re-integrated into the corporate memory as the kind of thing that Sense/Net had always known about, even though it had not. The fragment would still be there, in a sense, but it would be there in a different shape, behind a different door, and the three operators would have to start their whole intrusion over from scratch to find it.

He did this in eleven seconds. He had practiced. He had been practicing, in some form or another, since the day Sense/Net had issued him the credentials he was now using against them.

Denouement

Slick Henry called back at 0317. The Suit was already packing the bone-colored terminal back into the felt-lined drawer where it lived. "They pulled out," Slick said. "Whole suite, in the middle of a run. No explanation. They just — left."

"They will be back," the Suit said. "They are professional."

"They will be looking for the wrong thing."

"Yes," the Suit said.

He hung up. He sat for a moment in the Sprawl Hole, listening to the low hum of the corporate-statework machines around him. He had done a small thing. He had moved a fragment. The fragment was still there, in a sense, but the sense in which it was there was no longer the sense in which it had been there before. The three Cowboy-class operators would eventually find it again, or would not. Either way, Sense/Net would pay the Suit for his time, and the Suit would put the payment into the felt-lined drawer, next to the bone-colored terminal, and the Sprawl would go on being the Sprawl, which is to say: the place where small moves become large events, and large events become, in time, small moves.

He went back to his manifest. He had not been sleeping anyway. He did not intend to start.
