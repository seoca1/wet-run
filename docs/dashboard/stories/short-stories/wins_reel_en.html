Win's Reel

The reel had been, in Parkabrake's private count, the reel of a kind no longer made. The kind had been made in 1999. The 1999 had been the year Win had disappeared.

Win had been Win for thirty-eight years. The thirty-eight had been the years Parkabrake had known him.

The reel had arrived in the morning. The package had no return address. The address had been the address of a kind Win had always used.

Parkabrake held the reel. The reel was 16-millimeter. The 16-millimeter had been the 16-millimeter of a kind made by a factory in São Paulo.

Parkabrake threaded the reel. The threading had been the threading of a man who had threaded Win's reels for nine years.

The film ran. The images were Win's images. The images had been the images of a man who had disappeared in 1999.

The footage had come in 2002. The meaning had been, in his private count, the meaning of a man who had been working on something Win had already finished.

Parkabrake called Cayce.

"Tomorrow."

"I will be there."

"Bring no one."

"I will bring no one."

Parkabrake hung up. The reel ran out. The running out had been the running out of a man who had been missing for twenty-five years.

Parkabrake did not rewind the reel. The not rewinding had been the not rewinding of a man who did not, in any record he had been given, expect another reel.

He sat in the studio. The studio had been Win's studio. The studio had been the studio of a kind that did not, in any record he had been given, rent to anyone else.

He waited for Cayce.
