Marcus's Garage

A Party Driver Washes the Limo Before Dawn

289 wordsBridge Trilogy

The garage had been built in 1958 by a man who loved cars more than his wife. The man's son was the man Marcus drove for.

The limo sat on a pad poured for a Packard. The Packard had been sold in 1973. The pad had been waiting for the limo since.

Marcus washed the limo by hand. The hand-washing had been, in his private count, the only kind of washing that did not, in any record he had been given, scratch the lacquer. The lacquer had been applied in 1989 by a man who had since died.

The boss was inside the house. The boss had been inside for three days. The house had been the boss's house for the length of a marriage Marcus had driven to seven of its anniversaries.

The boss came out wearing a black jacket. The jacket was the kind his daughter had not yet approved.

"Dawn," the boss said. "The bridge."

"Yes," Marcus said.

"The bridge is moving."

"The bridge moves."

"Not like this."

Marcus did not answer. The boss went back inside. The boss had been going back inside since the day Marcus had been hired.

Marcus turned off the hose. The limo gleamed under a single bulb installed in 1961. The bulb had been, in his private count, the only kind of bulb that did not, in any record he had been given, flicker.

In the morning, the bridge would move. The bridge had been moving for as long as Marcus had been driving.

Marcus sat on a folding chair. The chair had been his father's chair. The father had been the father who had waited in this garage for engines to cool.

Marcus waited for dawn.
