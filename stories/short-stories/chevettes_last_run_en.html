Chevette's Last Run

1083 wordsBridge Trilogy

"Messengers don't ask what they're carrying. Messengers carry *because*."

— Virtual Light, Chevette's internal monologue

Introduction

The morning fog clung to the bridge like skin to bone, and Chevette Washington's breath came out in small silver clouds that hung for a moment before dissolving into the damp sodium-vapor glow of the Embarcadero streetlamps that had been burning since 1989 and would burn until the city decided to replace them with smart-matter luminaires that sensed pedestrian density and adjusted their output accordingly.

Chevette Washington pedaled her Bianchi up the Embarcadero, the messenger bag thumping against her hip with the rhythm of a small diesel engine, the single .38 round inside making soft clicking sounds with every bump.

The package was a hard drive. The customer was a law firm in SOMA. The route should have taken twenty minutes through the Saturday-morning fog that hadn't lifted since the dawn she'd started at her kitchen table, drinking coffee that had been sitting for an hour and was now cold in the cup.

Five blocks from the bridge approach, a black SUV cut her off. The door opened. Two men in suits — the kind of suits that don't wrinkle. The kind of suits that are made of materials she couldn't name.

"Ms. Washington." The taller one smiled. It was the smile of someone who knew her net worth. "We'd like the package."

Chevette's feet were on the ground. Her hands were on the handlebars. The messenger bag was on her hip.

Rising Action

"That depends on who's asking."

"The client's asking. The client's paying a lot to ensure the drive doesn't reach SOMA." The tall one's smile didn't waver. "The drive contains — but you don't care about contents, do you? You're a messenger. Messengers don't ask what they're carrying."

"Messengers don't ask what they're carrying." Chevette repeated the line. Let it settle. "But messengers do ask when someone cuts them off in traffic."

"The traffic is a metaphor. The fog is a metaphor. The bridge is a metaphor." The shorter one spoke for the first time. His voice was thin. Thin as in cheap. Thin as in kitchen knife. "What we want is the package."

"What's in the package?"

"The client's legal documents. A pending acquisition. A merger. The kind of merger that destroys small businesses in the Bay Area like yours." The shorter one stepped closer. "You have a small courier service. Two employees. One bike. The kind of service that doesn't exist in the virtual city. The kind of service that disappears when the merger goes through."

Chevette's fingers tightened on the handlebars. The messenger bag swung against her hip.

"And if I don't give you the package?"

"Then we take the package. And we take the Bianchi. And we take the bridge apartment you've been squatting on." The tall one's smile hardened. "We're very thorough."

Climax

"That's interesting." Chevette reached into her messenger bag. Not for the hard drive. For the .38 round. "You know what's interesting about carrying things from here to there?"

"We don't care."

"I'll tell you anyway." She held up the single round. It caught the morning light. "When you carry something physical, you learn what the physical world is for. The virtual city transmits data at the speed of light. The physical world carries things at the speed of a bike. The physical world is what happens when people decide the virtual one isn't enough."

The tall one blinked. The shorter one's knife voice slipped: "What are you—"

"I'm a messenger." Chevette's voice hardened. "I'm the difference between data and the world that holds the cables the data travels through. Without me, without the courier, the data has no substrate. Without substrate, the virtual city collapses. Without the virtual city, your client's merger —"

"Collateral," the shorter one whispered. "She's saying we're collateral."

"I'm saying the bridge is the substrate." Chevette pocketed the .38 round. "And the substrate doesn't sell."

The tall one made a hand signal. The black SUV's engine revved. The door stayed open.

"The client won't be pleased."

"The client won't know." Chevette got back on the Bianchi. "The client will get their merger. Their acquisition. Their virtual city. But the package arrives. The messages arrive. The messengers endure."

"The messengers endure?" The tall one's smile was back. The smile of someone who knew they couldn't win but had to try.

"The messengers endure." Chevette pulled her bike into the fog. The messenger bag swung. The Bianchi's chain clicked. "Because the physical world outlasts the virtual one. The physical world is what we protect."

She rode away. The black SUV didn't follow. The fog closed behind her.

Resolution

The law firm in SOMA had a wooden desk. The receptionist had a wooden smile. The hard drive exchanged hands without ceremony.

"Ms. Washington." The receptionist set down her coffee. "The client appreciates your discretion."

"The client's merger goes through tomorrow." Chevette didn't smile. "The merger kills three small businesses in the Bay Area. Two pizza places. One bike shop. The bike shop has been open since 1987."

"The client —"

"The client doesn't care." Chevette pulled out her ledger. The one without electronics. The paper one. "The client cares about the merge. The merge happens. But the messages endure."

She pocketed the payment. Cash. The receptionist didn't ask for a receipt.

Outside, the Bianchi waited. The bridge was visible from the firm's window — the fog burning off, the cables catching the light, the towers rising. The substrate. The world that held the cables.

Chevette pedaled home. The messenger bag was empty. The .38 round was back in her pocket. The bag's contents had been delivered. The bridge was still standing.

Somewhere a small bike shop in the Bay Area was about to close. Somewhere a virtual city was being built on top of a real one.

But the messengers endured. The physical world endured. The bridge endured.

Chevette didn't look back.

She never looked back.
