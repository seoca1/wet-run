Chiba Pulse

A Winter in the Working-Class Gaijin Enclave

1283 wordsSprawl Trilogy

The coffin was six feet long and smelled like someone else's sweat.

Case lay in it, the lid up, watching the wall. The wall was pressed against his shoulder and he was pressed against the wall opposite. Three centimeters of space for the entire human chest. The hotel had been built for traveling Japanese businessmen forty years ago. The businessmen had been replaced by the working-class diaspora of the post-industrial Pacific Rim. The diaspora had not been consulted about the redesign.

Slow. Deep.

He tasted the edge of it before Linda brought the dose. The particular slow-taste that preceded the slow-feel. The pulse of Chiba beating through the walls of the coffin. The pulse that was not the heart's pulse but the city's pulse. The pulse of an economy that had no patience for anyone who stopped.

Linda arrived at quarter past three in the afternoon of the second day. She came up the stairs of the coffin-hotel two at a time. Her shoes were inadequate for the stairs. She knocked on his door anyway. The door was a curtain of grey industrial canvas.

She said his name once. She said it twice.

He let her in.

She had brought the dose in a paper envelope from a clinic in Ninsei. The clinic did not ask her name. The clinic did not ask for a prescription. The clinic's reputation was that it did not ask questions. This reputation was correct. The dose came pre-measured. The dose came in a blister-pack stamped with the chemical name. The chemical name was a long word he did not try to remember.

He took the blister-pack.

He held the blister-pack against the light of the lamp. The lamp was a small lamp. The lamp was a small lamp because the coffin was a small coffin.

He said: I want to wait.

She said: for what.

He said: for the matrix.

She said: the matrix isn't going to come to Chiba.

He said: I know.

She said: you keep saying that.

He said: yes.

She looked at him for a long time. The light of the small lamp was yellow. The light of the small lamp was not the light of cyberspace. The light of cyberspace was not a light at all. The light of cyberspace was an absence of the small lamp.

She said: the envelope.

He took the envelope.

He said: I am going to wait.

She said: for what.

He said: I don't know yet.

She said: that is the same thing you said yesterday.

He said: it is.

She took the blister-pack from his hand. She set it on the small table beside the small lamp. The small table was the same table he used for the doses he did not take. The doses he did not take were the doses she had brought him before. The doses she had brought him before were the doses he had refused. The doses he had refused were the doses that had made him the way he was. The doses that had made him the way he was were the doses that had ruined his talent. The doses that had ruined his talent were the doses that he now refused.

He said: thank you.

She said: you are welcome.

She sat down on the edge of the coffin. The edge of the coffin was not designed for sitting. The edge of the coffin was designed for a body in a sleeping bag. The body in a sleeping bag was Case's body. Case's body was not currently in a sleeping bag. Case's body was sitting up against the wall.

She said: you are not going to take it.

He said: not tonight.

She said: not tonight. She repeated not tonight in a way that made not tonight into a calendar of all the not tonights that had accumulated between them. The not tonights that had accumulated between them were the not tonights of a man who had been a cowboy and was no longer a cowboy. The man who had been a cowboy and was no longer a cowboy was now a body in a coffin in Chiba. The body in a coffin in Chiba was waiting.

He said: do you want to stay.

She said: do you want me to stay.

He said: I want to ask if you want to stay.

She said: I want to stay.

She stayed.

The pulse of Chiba beat through the walls of the coffin. The pulse of Chiba was the pulse of a city that was a magnet for the techno-criminal subcultures of the Sprawl. The pulse of the city was the pulse of a working-class diaspora. The working-class diaspora was the diaspora of an industry that did not exist except as the pre-history of an industry that did not yet exist. The industry that did not yet exist was the industry of the next decade. The next decade had not yet arrived.

Case lay in the coffin beside her. The coffin was six feet long and smelled like someone else's sweat. The someone else was not Linda. The someone else was the previous occupant of the coffin. The previous occupant of the coffin had paid for a week of coffin and had not returned for the coffin.

Linda said: the city is louder tonight.

He said: it is always louder tonight.

She said: the noise.

He said: the noise.

She said: I cannot hear you.

He said: the noise.

She said: I cannot hear you.

He said: yes you can.

She turned her face toward the wall. The wall was pressed against his shoulder and he was pressed against the wall opposite. Three centimeters of space for the entire human chest. The entire human chest of two people was now contained in the six feet of coffin.

She said: do you know what year it is.

He said: yes.

She said: what year.

He said: I don't want to say the year.

She said: why not.

He said: because I am afraid of the year.

She said: I am afraid of the year too.

He said: the year is the year that I am no longer a cowboy.

She said: you were never a cowboy.

He said: I was a cowboy.

She said: you were a cowboy before you took the doses.

He said: the doses were the doses that the previous occupant of the coffin paid for.

She said: the previous occupant of the coffin is not the doses.

He said: the previous occupant of the coffin is the reason for the doses.

She said: the doses are the reason for you.

He said: the doses are the reason for the waiting.

She said: the waiting.

He said: the waiting.

She said: the waiting for the matrix.

He said: the matrix is not going to come to Chiba.

She said: I know.

He said: I know that you know.

She said: I know.

The pulse of Chiba beat through the walls of the coffin. The pulse of Chiba was the pulse of the waiting. The pulse of the waiting was the pulse of a body that did not know what it was waiting for. The body that did not know what it was waiting for was the body of a man who had been a cowboy. The man who had been a cowboy was now a body in a coffin.

The dose sat on the small table beside the small lamp.

He did not take it.

The pulse beat.

She stayed.
