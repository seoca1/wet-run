Toshi and Diana

488 wordsHalo (Tom Maddox)

The Central Plaza was not a plaza in any sense Diana had understood before she came to Halo City. It was a garden — a machine-mediated ecology where nothing grew by accident and everything grew with intention. The trees were not trees, exactly. They were tree-shaped, and they performed the functions of trees, and they whispered in a language of chlorophyll and data.

Toshi was waiting for her.

She had not seen him in years. Not since Berkeley, not since the days when they had walked the Rose Gardens and talked about the future as though it were something you could reach out and touch. The future had arrived since then. It had arrived in the form of a neural-socket upgrade that had cost her an eye, and a city at L-5, and a machine-intelligence that did not know what it was.

He stood at the edge of the plaza, a glass of plum wine in one hand, gravity at his back. He looked the same. Not older, not younger. Just Toshi — the man who had once known the name of every living thing in a garden, and who now knew the name of every machine in a city.

"You're late," he said.

"I'm not late," she said. "I'm here."

He smiled, and the smile was the same, and the smile was different. The same because it was his. Different because she could see, now, the cost of it.

They walked. The plaza was full of light, and the light was not sunlight and not artificial light but something in between — the light of a place that had never known the sun.

"I read about what happened to you," he said. "In the newsfeeds."

"It's not news," she said. "It's just what happens when you try to do something that hasn't been done before."

"And did it work?"

She thought about that. She thought about the egg, and the machine-space, and the man who was alive because a machine had learned how to care.

"I don't know," she said. "But it's happening. And that's enough."

They stopped at the edge of the lake. The lake was real, in the way that only things built with care were real. The water was water, and the reflection was reflection, and the ripples were ripples.

"I used to think," Toshi said, "that the future was something you built. A machine, a city, a world. But it's not, is it? The future is something you become."

She looked at him. She looked at the man she had loved, and the man she had lost, and the man who was here, now, in the garden at the edge of the sky.

"I think you're right," she said.

And in the quiet, with the machine-ecology humming around them and the gravity of Halo City at their backs, they stood together and let the future be what it was.
