Setup: The Buzz

K feels it in the back of his skull, a low-level buzz where the Ono-Sendai meets the cortex, the deck's bio-electrical interface degrading like an old Hosaka terminal on its second life. The Cyberspace 7 has been through three Chiba caps and one too many jack-outs. The Cyberspace 7 needs repair the way a working-class body needs water: not optional, not negotiable, not something that can wait for credit to materialize.

Rising: The Eighth Floor

The real Ono-Sendai service clinic is on the eighth floor. The brand office. The place where the Cyberspace 7's calibration would be performed with proprietary Ono-Sendai diagnostic rigs, where the deck would be recognized as Ono-Sendai, where the brand would do what the brand was built to do. K has the elevator fare. K does not have the credit. K goes up anyway. The rep is maybe thirty. The deck is older than the rep. K is younger than the rep. The rep says the kinds of things reps say — corporate client-rights, minimum-purchase thresholds, the Cyberspace 7's discontinued status.

Climax: The Cut-Off

The rep looks at the deck. Looks at K. Looks at the deck again. The rep does not say no. The rep says the things that mean no in a brand office: procedures, paperwork, the polite suggestion that K try the fourteenth floor. K has a brief life-flash moment. The brief life-flash moment is what working-class matrix-labor feel when the brand stops recognizing them. The deck is the working-class substrate. The brand office's non-recognition is the substrate's working-class cut-off.

Resolution: The Fourteenth

The street-corner jack-doc on the fourteenth is Pavel-equipment, no warranty, the technician who can fix a cracked interface but cannot tell a deck from a deck-chair. The technician fixes the cracked interface. The fix is partial. The deck runs at eighty percent of its designed capability. K is okay with eighty percent. Eighty percent is the working-class matrix-operative's standard operating capability. Eighty percent is what the Chiba port district's grifting economy can afford. Eighty percent is the substrate-pricing the era charges the working-class for the privilege of running at all.

Epilogue: The Rain

K walks out into the rain. The rain is still the same color as the data. The deck on K's head is still the same brand — Ono-Sendai. Just less of it. The brand office's non-recognition is what the working-class matrix-operatives experience as the era's substrate-pricing. K jacks in that night. The Cyberspace 7 is eighty percent. Eighty percent is enough for the next job. Eighty percent is enough for the next job because eighty percent is what the era gives the working-class to do the next job with.
