Parkabrake's Footage

A Location Scout on the Edges of the Bridge

259 wordsBlue Ant Trilogy

The bridge was above him. The bridge had been, in Parkabrake's private count, a bridge he had not crossed in three years.

He stood beneath it. The place did not, in any municipal record, exist. He had found it eleven years ago by accident. The accident had been, in his private count, the only kind of finding he trusted.

He had been hired for a shot.

The shot was a shot of a man the client could not name. The client was a woman who said the word client as if it were not a word she used often.

The shot would be from below. The shot would be at dusk. The dusk would be, in his private count, the dusk of a particular Tuesday in October.

He walked the site. It was cracked asphalt, the asphalt of a highway closed in 1989 when the bridge was built. The bridge had been built because the city had decided to build a bridge.

The man would walk from west to east. At the precise moment Parkabrake would mark, the man would pass beneath a single intact streetlamp.

He marked the lamp with a small disc, the disc of a system his former commanding officer had taught him.

He did not call the client. He did not call the man. He walked back to his truck, a truck that did not, in any municipal record, belong to him.

He drove east. The bridge was a bridge he would not cross tonight.
