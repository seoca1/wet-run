Alpha Centauri: Flynne Looks Back from 2099

Origin: Flynne's first trip to the Alpha Centauri colony

906 wordsPeripheral

The sky was the wrong color.

That was the first thing I noticed when the peripheral's body stepped off the surface transport and the helmet visor cycled from gold to clear. The sky was the wrong color. Not blue. Not the kind of gray-blue that Clanton got on a January afternoon when the clouds were low and you couldn't tell if it was going to rain or just sulk. The sky here was a pale, almost violet, the color of a bruise that's a few weeks old and has decided to stay.

Conner was beside me. He was wearing a different peripheral body from the one he'd had in London — taller, broader, with a faint dusting of something across the upper lip that I understood to be a stylistic choice rather than a medical condition. He was looking up at the sky too.

"Four point three light years," he said.

"Four point three light years from where."

"From Sol."

"Sol is the sun."

"Yes."

"The sun is the sun I grew up under. The sun I grew up under was yellow."

"The sun here is the same sun, Ms. Fisher. It's the atmosphere that changes the color. The colony at Alpha Centauri runs on a different atmospheric mix than Earth. Slightly more argon. Slightly more of a trace gas they call pollin, which is not what you would think it is. The combination shifts the spectrum into the violet."

"Violet."

"Yes."

"The sky where I grew up was yellow, and the sky here is violet, and the sun is the same sun."

"Yes."

"Conner."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"I am standing on a planet that is four point three light years from the planet where I was born."

"Yes."

"And I am standing on it in a body that is not mine."

"Yes."

"So the body that is mine is in a trailer in Clanton, West Virginia, sitting in a pod that smells like iodine, and the body that is not mine is standing on a planet that is four point three light years from the planet where I was born, and both of those things are happening at the same time, and one of them is more real than the other and I do not know which one."

"The body in Clanton is more real," Conner said. "That's the agreement. The peripheral body is the temporary one. Yours is the primary one. You can always come back."

"I can always come back to the trailer."

"You can always come back to the trailer."

"And if the body that is not mine dies on this planet that is four point three light years from the planet where I was born."

"Then you wake up in the trailer."

"And someone on this end has to build a new body."

"Yes."

"How long does that take."

"A few weeks."

"A few weeks in which I am a person without a body that is not mine."

"Yes."

I looked up at the violet sky.

I looked down at the ground. The ground was a kind of reddish tan that I had never seen before. There were small plants growing out of it in low clusters, each cluster the color of the inside of an avocado, and a faint smell that was both mineral and sweet, the way a creek bed smells after a rain but with something else layered on top of it, something that didn't have a name in English and that I would not have a name in English for a long time, because what they were smelling was pollin and pollin didn't have a name in English yet because pollin had only been discovered on this colony four years ago and the English name was still being decided.

The plants were not plants. They were something else. They were something that had been engineered to grow in the pollin-rich soil of this particular planet, and they had been engineered by people who had not yet decided what to call them in any language.

"Conner."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"What do you call those." I pointed at the lowest cluster.

"We call them carpets."

"You call them carpets."

"Yes."

"You are standing on a planet that is four point three light years from Earth, looking at a thing you call a carpet that grows in pollin-rich soil, under a violet sky, and you are doing all of that in a body that is not yours that was built for you by someone whose name you have not told me."

"Yes."

I bent down.

I touched the carpet with the peripheral's hand.

It was cool. It was slightly damp. It gave under my finger the way a wet sponge gives, but slowly, like it was deciding whether to give.

I stood back up.

The violet sky hadn't changed.

The sun hadn't changed.

The body I was standing in hadn't changed.

The body I was not standing in, the body that was mine, the body that was in the trailer in Clanton West Virginia sitting in a pod that smelled like iodine, was breathing on its own, in its own time, in its own place, and would still be breathing when I came back to it.

"Conner."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"It's beautiful."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

"It's beautiful and I want to go home."

"Yes, Ms. Fisher."

I looked at the violet sky for a long time.

Then we went home.
